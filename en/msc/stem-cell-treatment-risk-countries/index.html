Safety and Regulation of Stem Cell Therapy Abroad - Which Countries Should Be Avoided?

14 Maret 2025
  • Stem Cell Therapy & Regenerative Care

There has been a growing interest in stem cell therapy options in recent years. Notably, while therapies utilizing autologous stem cells are mainstream in Japan, the number of individuals seeking therapies with younger stem cells continues to rise.

However, to undergo therapy utilizing young allogeneic stem cells, it is necessary to select a medical facility abroad. Consequently, many individuals find it challenging to make an informed decision due to the limited reliable information available regarding the optimal country choice.

Therefore, this article discusses Southeast Asian countries providing stem cell therapy, with a particular focus on those requiring special attention to safety.

Increase in Illegal Clinics: Thailand

When you search for "Thailand stem cell," news articles with titles such as "An Increasing Number of Foreigners to the Only Country in the World Where Stem Cell Therapy is Possible" frequently rank highly.

However, many of these articles are based on information from 2007 and may differ from the current situation. When searching for information on overseas stem cell therapy in Japanese, the information is often not up-to-date, so careful verification is necessary.

Previously, serious adverse events related to stem cell therapy occurred in Thailand, leading the government to subsequently prohibit stem cell therapy for conditions other than specific diseases (leukemia, multiple myeloma, congenital immunodeficiency, and other blood cancers).

Consequently, there are no unified safety standards concerning stem cell cultivation, and cases have been reported where imported stem cells were utilized.

Additionally, regarding stem cell harvesting, donor eligibility is unclear, and in some areas, concerns exist about potential involvement in illegal organ trafficking.

Furthermore, excessive advertising related to stem cell clinics in Thailand has become an issue, and according to a report dated October 31, 2024, over 400 lawsuits are reportedly filed annually (Source: Nation Thailand).

In Thailand, the unauthorized provision of stem cell therapy has become a significant issue. In 2024, the government is actively identifying unlicensed stem cell clinics and issuing closure orders for facilities found to be engaging in illegal medical practices. According to the Thai Ministry of Public Health (MOPH), stem cell therapy is restricted to "leukemia, multiple myeloma, congenital immunodeficiency, and other blood cancers," and treatments for other diseases may be deemed illegal.

Sumber: Ministry of Public Health of Thailand (MOPH)

POIN

Currently, stem cell therapy in Thailand is only recognized for the following symptoms:

  • Leukemia
  • Multiple Myeloma
  • Primary Immunodeficiency
  • Other Blood Cancers

Other Countries Where Private Stem Cell Therapy Is Considered Illegal

Even in countries where legal regulations were previously ambiguous, there are now instances where the provision of stem cell therapy is explicitly deemed illegal. In these nations, uncertified medical facilities and companies may attempt to offer such therapies, making it essential to ensure appropriate legal oversight.

Additionally, cases have been reported where some Japanese businesses and clinics advertised stem cell therapy overseas, unaware of its illegality. To receive appropriate treatment, it is crucial to thoroughly investigate the latest legal regulations of the provider country.

Korea

In Korea, the cultivation of stem cells is prohibited outside of certified pharmaceutical companies.

Consequently, there are concerns that illegally sourced stem cells may be circulating on the international market, necessitating vigilance. Furthermore, reports indicate unauthorized treatments being performed on foreign nationals.

In South Korea, the cultivation and production of stem cells are regulated by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS), and only licensed pharmaceutical companies and designated medical facilities are authorized to perform these activities. Unauthorized cultivation or sale of stem cells is considered a violation of the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act, and offenders may be subject to penalties.

Sumber: Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS)

Taiwan

In Taiwan, the Regenerative Medicine Act and regulations for regenerative medicine products were enacted in 2024, establishing a clear legal framework.

  • Implementasi di fasilitas medis sangat penting:
    Terapi sel punca hanya diperbolehkan disediakan di fasilitas medis yang memiliki sertifikasi yang sesuai.
  • Persetujuan pemerintah dan kewajiban pendaftaran:
    Persetujuan dari Departemen Kesejahteraan Kesehatan diperlukan, dan pendaftaran lebih lanjut dengan otoritas yurisdiksi lokal diperlukan.
  • Standar kualitas produk terapi sel punca:
    Produk perawatan regeneratif diklasifikasikan sebagai obat-obatan, dan manufaktur di fasilitas berstandar GMP diwajibkan. Ketika diproduksi atau diimpor oleh pembuat obat, aplikasi pemeriksaan dan pendaftaran ke Departemen Kesejahteraan Kesehatan diperlukan.

In Taiwan, the "Regenerative Medicine Act" and the "Regenerative Medicine Product Regulations" were promulgated in June 2024. Under these laws, the provision of stem cell therapy is restricted to government-approved medical facilities, and its provision in non-medical facilities is deemed illegal. To administer stem cell therapy, approval from the Ministry of Health and Welfare and subsequent registration with local jurisdictional authorities are mandatory.

Regenerative medicine products are classified as pharmaceuticals, and manufacturing in GMP-compliant facilities is mandatory. When manufactured or imported, applications for inspection and registration with the Department of Health and Welfare are essential, and violations may be subject to sanctions.

Sumber: Taiwan Ministry of Health and Welfare (MOHW)

Indonesia

The Indonesian government is formulating standards to ensure the safety of stem cell therapy.

  • Fasilitas penyedia terapi sel punca:
    Pada saat ini, hanya rumah sakit pendidikan negara yang dapat melakukan terapi sel punca, dan terapi sel punca swasta dianggap ilegal.
  • Tindakan khusus:
    Sebuah zona ekonomi medis telah didirikan di distrik Sanur di Bali, dan ada kemungkinan bahwa teknologi sel punca dari luar negeri akan diperkenalkan di bawah kondisi tertentu.

In Indonesia, the provision of stem cell therapy is restricted by law and regulation exclusively to state-affiliated teaching hospitals. Pursuant to Ministry of Health Regulation No. 32, enacted in 2014, medical facilities authorized to provide stem cell therapy are limited to state-designated hospitals, and its provision in general private clinics is deemed illegal.

Furthermore, a medical economic zone has been established in the Sanur district of Bali, with the potential for international stem cell technology to be introduced under specific conditions.

Sumber: Indonesian Ministry of Health (Kemenkes)

Filipina

The Philippine government has implemented stringent regulations to ensure the safety of stem cell therapy.

  • Hanya klinik sel punca yang tersusun dalam rumah sakit besar yang diizinkan:
    Fasilitas yang melakukan terapi sel punca terbatas pada fasilitas medis yang telah lulus pemeriksaan ketat.
  • Kerusakan kesehatan dari perawatan ilegal:
    Ada laporan kematian dari perawatan di klinik swasta ilegal, dan pemerintah mendesak publik untuk berhati-hati.

The Philippine government issued Administrative Order AO 2013-0012 in 2013, restricting the provision of stem cell therapy solely to government-recognized facilities. To administer stem cell therapy, facilities must be medical facilities recognized by the Philippine Department of Health (DOH), and its provision in any other clinic is deemed illegal.

Reports have emerged concerning adverse health effects linked to illicit stem cell therapies. Notably, in 2019, fatalities were confirmed among patients who had undergone uncertified treatments. The Philippine government maintains stringent enforcement against unauthorized procedures, and violators are subject to sanctions.

Sumber: Philippine Department of Health (DOH)

Vietnam

Although a comprehensive legal framework for stem cell therapy in Vietnam is not yet fully established, a statement has been released declaring it illegal, with the exception of specific clinical trials and research.

  • Dalam kasus uji coba klinis/penelitian:
    Diperlukan untuk lulus tinjauan, evaluasi, dan persetujuan dari Komite Etika Riset Biomedis Nasional.
  • Perawatan sel punca tanpa izin dilarang:
    Penyediaan terapi sel punca komersial tidak diizinkan saat ini.

Although a clear legal framework for stem cell therapy in Vietnam has not been fully established, the provision of commercial stem cell therapy without the approval of the National Biomedical Research Ethics Committee is prohibited.

Currently, stem cell therapy is recognized solely as clinical research, and its unlicensed provision may constitute a violation of the Medical Act. In 2023, a clinic operating unlicensed stem cell therapy was apprehended, and the government is strengthening regulations.

Sumber: Ministry of Health of Vietnam (MOH)

In this manner, many countries have established stringent legal regulations and associated safety systems for stem cell therapy to protect public health.

On the other hand, a Japanese entrepreneur provides stem cell therapy that is not approved by any country, by importing the cells from abroad. However, in many countries, such practices are considered illegal, and careful consideration is required.

To Safely Undergo Stem Cell Therapy Abroad - Malaysia

Currently, in Asia, only Japan and Malaysia permit the safe administration of stem cell therapy.

Although autologous stem cell therapy is prevalent in Japan, for those seeking treatment with young and healthy Wharton's Jelly stem cells, Malaysia presents an optimal choice.

While there is no specific legislation directly regulating stem cell therapy treatments, regulations concerning safety and guidelines for care are in place. Under government oversight, it is possible to receive treatment in an environment where safety and quality are assured.

Guide to Stem Cell and Cell-Based Research and Therapy (3rd Edition)

Furthermore, the provision of stem cell therapy by unlicensed medical facilities may violate the Private Healthcare Facilities and Services Act 1998 (PHFS Act 1998) and be subject to regulation by supervisory authorities, thereby enabling the receipt of treatment in an environment where safety and quality are assured under government oversight.

In Malaysia, the regulation of stem cell therapy is governed by the "Guidelines for Stem Cell and Cell-Based Research and Therapy" established in 2009, and stem cell therapy may only be performed in licensed medical facilities. The use of human embryonic stem cells is prohibited, and treatment with mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) is carefully managed as clinical research.

To conduct stem cell therapy, approval must be obtained from the National Stem Cell Ethics Research Committee (NSCERT), which operates under the purview of the Ministry of Health Malaysia (MOH). Additionally, the quality management of stem cell therapy products is overseen by the National Pharmaceutical Regulatory Agency (NPRA), and adherence to Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards is mandatory.

Sumber: Ministry of Health Malaysia (MOH)

In Malaysia, the use of hematopoietic stem cells and umbilical cord-derived stem cells (including Wharton's Jelly stem cells) is recognized. Facilities providing stem cell therapy must obtain approval from the Ministry of Health (MOH) and the National Stem Cell Research Ethics Sub-Committee (NSCERT). The provision of stem cell therapy by unlicensed medical facilities may contravene the Private Healthcare Facilities and Services Act 1998 (PHFS Act 1998) and be subject to regulation by supervisory authorities.

Sumber: National Pharmaceutical Regulatory Agency (NPRA)
