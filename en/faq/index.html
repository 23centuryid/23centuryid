Frequently Asked Questions

When you first consider stem cell therapy and regenerative treatments, we have compiled frequently asked questions and their answers.
We try to explain technical terms as simply as possible so that they are easy to understand.

  • What is Regenerative Therapy?

    Regenerative therapy is a medical discipline that aims to repair or replace cells and tissues damaged or lost due to disease or injury. In regenerative therapy, new cells are introduced to restore physiological function. By enhancing the body's natural healing capabilities, this approach seeks to improve conditions previously considered incurable.

    Specific methods include stem cell therapy (which we provide), tissue engineering techniques (the creation of artificial tissues and organs), and gene therapy.

  • What are the applications of regenerative therapy?

    Regenerative therapy has extensive applications. Examples include brain and spinal cord injuries, cardiac damage following a heart attack, ocular diseases such as corneal blindness or retinal diseases, and articular cartilage damage.

    Research and applications continue to advance for neurological diseases such as Parkinson's and Alzheimer's diseases, autoimmune conditions like type 1 diabetes, severe dermatological disorders, and recalcitrant wounds. Additionally, regenerative therapies are being explored for cancer treatment (immunotherapy) and the prevention of aging (anti-aging).

  • What is Stem Cell Therapy?

    Stem cell therapy is a medical treatment modality that utilizes specialized stem cells to restore physiological function. Stem cells possess two crucial capabilities: self-renewal (the capacity for self-replication) and differentiation (the capacity to develop into diverse cell types).

    These cells are found in various parts of the body, including bone marrow, blood, adipose tissue, and the umbilical cord (which we utilize). They play a role in repairing damaged tissue and replacing lost cells. Stem cell therapy can utilize cells from the patient's own body (autologous) or from a donor (allogeneic).

  • What is NK Cell Therapy?

    NK cell therapy is a therapeutic approach that involves activating and expanding natural killer (NK) cells within the body, followed by their reinfusion into the patient to bolster immune function. NK cells are a class of immune cells capable of identifying and targeting cancer cells or virus-infected cells.

    When NK cell function declines due to disease or aging, the body's ability to combat cancer cells weakens, and susceptibility to infection increases. By reactivating NK cells, this therapy can enhance the body's defenses against cancer and infectious diseases.

  • What is Secretome?

    Secretome is defined as the fluid secreted by stem cells during culture, which is rich in beneficial components. This fluid comprises cytokines, growth factors, and other bioactive molecules that contribute to reducing inflammation and fostering cellular repair and regeneration.

    Exosomes are a component found within the secretome and are responsible for intercellular communication. The secretome contains various active components, enabling broader applications in medicine.

    In the field of regenerative therapy, the use of secretome is relatively novel and is currently being applied primarily for skin rejuvenation, wound healing, and joint pain reduction.

  • How long do the effects of the treatment last?

    The onset and duration of effects vary depending on the type of disease and individual patient differences. Some patients report pain reduction and symptom improvement immediately after treatment.

    For example, in patients with rheumatoid arthritis who received direct intra-articular stem cell injections, swelling subsided and pain was reduced immediately following treatment.

    Conversely, cases involving nerve repair or organ regeneration typically require several months for the effects to be observed.

    Approximately 3-6 months post-treatment, stem cells typically commence optimal functioning, at which point significant symptomatic improvements are frequently observed. This timeframe is necessary as stem cells require time to adapt within the body and differentiate into the requisite cell types, leading to a gradual progression of physiological changes.

    For instance, in cases of spinal cord injury, muscle strength begins to improve 6 months post-treatment, and ambulation continues to enhance over the subsequent year. Clinical studies and trials also indicate that the effects of stem cell therapy tend to augment over time.

    It is crucial to observe the effects patiently, consistently continuing physical therapy and routine examinations as your body recovers.

  • Are there any side effects of stem cell therapy?

    Stem cell therapy is considered a treatment with a very low incidence of side effects. Globally, there are few reported instances of serious, life-threatening adverse events following stem cell infusion.

    In clinical trials and 23Century treatment, no serious adverse events were detected. Possible side effects include transient fever, headache, and nausea.

    In some cases, a mild fever may develop within 24-48 hours post-infusion, indicative of a mild immune response. Headache and fatigue may also occur, but these symptoms typically resolve within a few days.

    This is regarded as a transient reaction that occurs upon the body's reception of stem cells. Additionally, in extremely rare instances, localized pain or a rash at the injection site may manifest; however, with appropriate management, resolution is typically swift.

    It is important to monitor your physical condition post-treatment and promptly contact medical staff if you experience anything unusual.

  • Will my genetic material be altered if allogeneic stem cells are administered?

    Your genes and genetic material will not be altered if another person's stem cells are injected into you.

    The therapeutic effect of stem cell therapy does not arise from the direct integration of transplanted stem cells into tissues or the rewriting of the patient's genetic information. Instead, it is due to the transplanted stem cells activating the patient's own cells and promoting functional recovery. Consequently, DNA and genes are not altered by stem cell injection.

    Initially, it was believed that transplanted stem cells migrated to the site of injury and integrated into that tissue to differentiate into specific cells that exerted therapeutic effects. However, recent research indicates that the number of transplanted cells remaining at the injury site long-term is very small, and the primary mechanism of the therapeutic effect is that stem cells secrete biologically active substances that heal the patient's own cells.

    Therefore, stem cell therapy is safe because the genes are not altered.
