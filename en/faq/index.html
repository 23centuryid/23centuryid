  • What is Regenerative Therapy?

    Regenerative therapy is a medical field focused on repairing or replacing cells and tissues damaged or lost due to disease or injury. In regenerative therapy, new cells are introduced to restore physiological function. By enhancing the body's natural healing capabilities, this approach aims to improve conditions previously considered incurable.

    Specific methods include stem cell therapy (which we provide), tissue engineering techniques (the fabrication of artificial tissues and organs), and gene therapy.

  • What are the applications of Regenerative Therapy?

    Regenerative therapy has wide-ranging applications. Examples include brain and spinal cord injuries, cardiac damage following a heart attack, ocular diseases such as corneal blindness or retinal diseases, and articular cartilage damage.

    Research and applications are continuously advancing for neurological diseases such as Parkinson's and Alzheimer's diseases, autoimmune diseases like type 1 diabetes, severe dermatological conditions, and chronic wounds. Furthermore, regenerative therapies are also being explored for cancer treatment (immunotherapy) and aging prevention (anti-aging).

  • What is Stem Cell Therapy?

    Stem cell therapy is a medical treatment modality that utilizes specialized stem cells to restore physiological function. Stem cells possess two fundamental properties: self-renewal (the capacity for self-replication) and differentiation (the ability to differentiate into diverse cell types).

    These cells are found in various parts of the body, including bone marrow, blood, adipose tissue, and umbilical cord blood, which is a source we utilize. They play a crucial role in repairing damaged tissue and replacing lost cells. Stem cell therapy can utilize cells from the patient (autologous) or from a donor (allogeneic).

  • What is NK Cell Therapy?

    NK cell therapy is a treatment method that involves activating and expanding natural killer (NK) cells from the body, followed by their reinfusion into the patient to enhance immune function. NK cells are a type of immune cell capable of identifying and targeting cancer cells or virus-infected cells.

    When NK cell function declines due to disease or aging, the body's ability to combat cancer cells weakens, and susceptibility to infections increases. By reactivating NK cells, this therapy can enhance the body's defenses against cancer and infectious diseases.

  • What is Secretome?

    The secretome is a fluid rich in beneficial components secreted by stem cells during culture. This fluid contains cytokines, growth factors, and other active compounds that aid in reducing inflammation and promote cellular repair and regeneration.

    Exosomes are a constituent of the secretome that mediate intercellular communication. The secretome itself comprises diverse active components, enabling a wider range of therapeutic applications.

    In the field of regenerative therapy, the use of secretome is relatively novel and is being applied primarily for skin rejuvenation, wound healing, and the reduction of joint pain.

  • How long do the effects of the treatment last?

    The onset and duration of effects vary depending on the specific disease type and individual patient variability. A subset of patients report immediate pain alleviation and symptomatic improvement following treatment.

    For example, in patients with rheumatoid arthritis who received stem cell injections directly into the joint, swelling subsided and pain was reduced immediately following treatment.

    On the other hand, cases involving neural repair or organ regeneration typically require several months for the effects to become apparent.

    As an estimate, approximately 3-6 months post-treatment, stem cells begin to operate at their full potential, and significant symptomatic improvements are often observed. Stem cells require time to adapt within the body and differentiate into the necessary cell types, consequently, physiological changes occur gradually.

    For instance, in cases of spinal cord injury, muscle strength begins to improve 6 months post-treatment, and walking ability continues to enhance over the subsequent year. Clinical studies and trials also indicate that the effects of stem cell therapy tend to augment over time.

    It is essential to observe the effects patiently, continuing with physical therapy and routine examinations as your body recovers.

  • Are there any side effects of stem cell therapy?

    Stem cell therapy is considered a treatment with a very low incidence of side effects. Globally, there have been few reports of serious (life-threatening) adverse events associated with stem cell infusion.

    In 23Century clinical trials and treatments, no serious adverse events were detected. Possible side effects include transient fever, headache, and nausea.

    In some cases, mild fever may occur within 24-48 hours post-infusion as a mild immune reaction, or headache and fatigue may be experienced; these symptoms typically resolve within a few days.

    This is considered a transient reaction that occurs when the body receives stem cells. Additionally, in very rare cases, localized pain or rash may occur at the injection site; however, with appropriate management, resolution occurs rapidly.

    It is important to monitor your physical condition for a period following treatment and to promptly contact medical staff if you experience any unusual symptoms.

  • Is the Secretome different from Stem Cells?

    Yes, they are different.

    The secretome is a fluid containing beneficial growth factors and cytokines secreted by stem cells when cultured, and is devoid of live stem cells.

    Treatment options vary depending on the objective. However, for severe diseases or when extensive tissue regeneration is required, the direct application of stem cells themselves is more effective. Conversely, for relatively mild conditions aimed at reducing inflammation, or for aesthetic purposes (e.g., skin rejuvenation), the simpler method of infusing secretome is often chosen.

  • Will my genes change if allogeneic stem cells are injected?

    Your genes and genetic material will not be altered if stem cells from another person are injected into you.

    The therapeutic effect of stem cell therapy is not due to the transplanted stem cells being directly integrated into tissues or rewriting the patient's genetic information. Instead, it is because the transplanted stem cells activate the patient's own cells and promote functional recovery. Consequently, DNA and genes are not altered by stem cell injections.

    Initially, it was believed that transplanted stem cells migrated to the site of injury and integrated into the tissue to differentiate into specific cells that exerted therapeutic effects. However, recent research indicates that the number of transplanted cells remaining at the injury site long-term is very small, and the primary mechanism of the therapeutic effect is that stem cells secrete biologically active substances that heal the patient's own cells.

    Therefore, stem cell therapy is safe because the genes are not altered.
