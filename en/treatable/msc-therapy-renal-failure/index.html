Kidney Failure and Its Treatment - Why 'Current Treatment Alone Is Not Enough'?

Dengan terapi standar seperti dialisis dan transplantasi ginjal, tidak semua kasus gagal ginjal yang progresif dapat ditangani, dan banyak pasien merasakan keterbatasan dalam kualitas hidup dan pilihan pengobatan.

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is a silently progressing disease, also known as the "silent disease," and is estimated to affect over 13 million people. When renal function significantly declines to End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD), renal replacement therapy (RRT) is required, such as hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, or kidney transplantation.

However, in actual clinical practice, the number of patients undergoing dialysis continues to increase annually, with over 330,000 individuals undergoing chronic dialysis therapy by 2017. Conversely, the number of kidney transplants, which is a curative treatment, has remained below 1,700 cases per year, leaving many patients who desire transplantation unable to receive one.

Underlying this situation is a severe donor shortage. Furthermore, although dialysis therapy is a vital means of life support, the following burdens on daily life are unavoidable:

  • Regular visits three times per week, with sessions lasting 4-5 hours.
  • Sustained food and fluid restriction
  • Risk of complications such as infection and cardiovascular disease

Kidney transplantation also presents challenges, such as donor procurement issues, rejection, adverse effects of immunosuppressive medications, and the substantial financial burden of medical expenses.

Conservative management, comprising dietary and pharmacological interventions, can decelerate disease progression. However, it lacks the capacity to restore intrinsic renal function once it has declined. Essentially, within the current medical framework, "we can arrest deterioration but cannot revert the kidneys to their original healthy state."

Against this backdrop, there is a strong demand for novel treatment strategies that can replace or complement dialysis and transplantation.

Item

  • CKD is considered a "new national disease," affecting approximately 1 in 8 adults.
  • Dialysis and transplantation are important treatments, but they cannot meet the needs of all patients.
  • Under current treatment modalities, it is extremely challenging to 'restore' the intrinsic function of the kidney.
  • Novel treatment methods are necessary to broaden quality of life and therapeutic options.

Potential curative therapies for renal failure are beginning to emerge.

Dengan kemajuan kedokteran regeneratif, Terapi Sel Punca yang bertujuan untuk pemulihan fungsi ginjal itu sendiri telah menjadi pilihan baru yang berbeda dari dialisis dan transplantasi dengan kemungkinan praktis.

Treatment for renal failure has traditionally been dominated by substitutive therapies such as dialysis and kidney transplantation. However, with recent advancements in regenerative medicine, the concept of repairing the kidney itself has become a reality.

Special attention is given to stem cell therapy.

Stem cells are unique cells possessing the "self-renewal capability" to produce identical copies of themselves, and the "pluripotency" to differentiate into other cell types as required. By leveraging these capabilities, the fundamental principle of Stem Cell Therapy is to regenerate damaged organs and tissues.

In the field of nephrology, mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) have garnered particular attention. MSCs can be obtained from sources such as bone marrow, adipose tissue, and umbilical cord, and possess various therapeutic actions including anti-inflammatory effects, immune modulation, and tissue repair.

Preclinical (animal) studies and early clinical trials have demonstrated that MSCs can modulate immune responses, reduce inflammation-induced renal damage, and contribute to the preservation and enhancement of renal function.

Characteristics of Wharton's Jelly-derived MSCs (WJ-MSC)

Among the various MSCs, umbilical cord-derived stem cells (WJ-MSCs) are considered the most promising.

WJ-MSC are cells derived from a gelatinous tissue known as 'Wharton's Jelly,' found within the umbilical cord which is discarded after birth, and possess the following characteristics:

  • Young and Active Cells

    Karena berasal dari bayi baru lahir, sel-sel tersebut muda, dengan kapasitas proliferasi yang lebih tinggi daripada yang berasal dari sumsum tulang atau lemak, dan sekresi faktor pertumbuhan yang lebih besar.

  • Slightly rejected

    Ekspresi antigen yang terlibat dalam imunitas rendah, sehingga ketika diberikan kepada orang lain, tidak mudah menimbulkan reaksi penolakan.

  • Minor Ethical Issue

    Tidak ada beban bagi donor pada saat pengambilan, dan karena diperoleh dari tali pusat yang secara alami dibuang setelah lahir, ini adalah bahan yang relatif mudah diterima secara etis.

  • A stable supply is achievable.

    Budaya dan proliferasi di luar tubuh mudah dilakukan, dan jumlah besar dapat dijamin, yang sesuai untuk aplikasi klinis.

WJ-MSC, as "readily available, high-quality, allogeneic cells," has emerged as a prime candidate for Stem Cell Therapy in renal failure.

Similarly in Japan, several medical institutions have begun implementing Stem Cell Therapy using WJ-MSC as a private treatment option, garnering attention as an approach distinct from traditional kidney transplantation.

Item

  • Stem Cell Therapy is a form of regenerative medicine that aims to heal the kidney itself.
  • MSC excels in immune modulation, anti-inflammatory activity, and tissue repair, with the potential to improve renal function.
  • WJ-MSC is young, active, minimally immunogenic, and has a stable supply.
  • As a novel therapeutic strategy that does not require donor organs, it may serve as an alternative to renal transplantation.

How Do Stem Cells Work in the Kidneys?

MSC (sel punca mesenchymal) memiliki berbagai tindakan dalam ginjal yang menenangkan lingkungan peradangan ginjal, melindungi sel yang rusak, dan mempromosikan perbaikan dan regenerasi.

Underlying the effectiveness of Stem Cell Therapy in treating renal failure are various biological actions demonstrated by MSCs administered intrarenally.

Specifically, Wharton's Jelly-derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells (WJ-MSCs) are reported to possess the capacity to "suppress inflammation, protect cells, and facilitate repair," thereby addressing the fundamental issues underlying renal damage.

Below, its main actions are explained from three perspectives.

Calming Inflammation: Immunomodulation and Anti-Inflammatory Actions

Perkembangan penyakit ginjal kronis dan gagal ginjal terkait erat dengan reaksi peradangan berlebihan dan kelainan imun.
MSC bertindak sebagai rem untuk "imunitas yang lari liar" ini dan melakukan penyesuaian berikut:

  • Inhibiting the activity of pro-inflammatory immune cells, such as T cells and macrophages.
  • Decreasing the release of inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α)
  • Inhibiting apoptosis and suppressing tissue destruction

In this manner, MSC plays a role in mitigating damage caused by renal inflammation and establishing a quiescent environment where repair can proceed.

Protecting Renal Tissue: Restorative and Anti-Fibrotic Support

Ketika ginjal rusak, area tersebut seiring waktu digantikan oleh jaringan parut yang keras (jaringan fibrotik). Ini berarti penurunan fungsi ginjal yang tidak dapat diperbalikkan.
MSC menunjukkan tindakan berikut terhadap perkembangan ini:

  • Releasing growth factors (VEGF, HGF, IGF-1, etc.) and promoting blood vessel regeneration (angiogenesis).
  • Supporting the survival and proliferation of damaged cells
  • Secreting fibrosis-suppressing cytokines (e.g., by inhibiting TGF-β).

This allows for an approach to "prevent scar tissue formation and protect native functioning renal tissue."

Protection from Oxidative Stress: Antioxidant Action

In chronic kidney disease, oxidative stress caused by reactive oxygen species (ROS) is also a significant contributor to cellular damage. MSCs promote the production of antioxidant compounds and enhance cellular defenses.

Specifically, the exosomes (extracellular vesicles) released by umbilical cord-derived WJ-MSCs contain numerous molecules that suppress the production of reactive oxygen species. Animal experiments have confirmed the suppression of oxidative stress and the protection of renal cells.

What is the Secretome? - Characterization of Stem Cell-Derived Bioactive Factors

The majority of the effects of Stem Cell Therapy on the kidneys are not due to the MSCs themselves differentiating into renal cells. Instead, various factors secreted by MSCs act upon renal cells, functioning through paracrine actions (local secretion) that promote self-repair.

This collection of secreted factors is referred to as the secretome.

The secretome includes the following components:

  • Cytokines and growth factors (e.g., HGF, VEGF, IL-10, etc.)
  • Anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic molecules
  • Small extracellular vesicles (such as exosomes)

In other words, MSCs function as a living pharmaceutical factory, delivering the requisite tools for repair to the compromised renal environment.

Item

  • MSC suppresses dysregulated immune responses and reduces renal inflammation.
  • Supports the repair of damaged cells and protects tissues by preventing fibrosis.
  • Combating oxidative stress and reducing renal cell death
  • The primary action is based on the paracrine effect by the secretome.

Can Kidney Failure Be Reversed? Prospects Seen Through Stem Cell Therapy

Dari eksperimen hewan dan uji klinis, telah dilaporkan data yang menunjukkan bahwa pemeliharaan dan pemulihan fungsi ginjal dapat diharapkan melalui Terapi Sel Punca.

Renal Protective Measures Confirmed in Animal Models

In animal model studies where nephritis or renal failure was induced, numerous instances have been reported where MSC administration suppressed inflammation and fibrosis, and both renal structure and function improved.

Observed Signs of Improvement in Clinical Trials

In a randomized controlled trial targeting diabetic nephropathy, allogeneic MSCs were administered once intravenously to patients with moderate to severe CKD. Compared to the placebo group, the following trends were observed:

  • 12 weeks post-administration, eGFR demonstrated a mean improvement trend of +4 mL/min/1.73㎡.
  • The placebo group showed a decrease in eGFR.
  • Potential cessation of functional decline
  • No serious adverse effects have been reported in association with the administration.

Examples of Improvements in End-Stage Renal Disease Patients

In the case of a 70-year-old male (CKD Stage V) who received an intravenous infusion of Wharton's Jelly-derived mesenchymal stem cells (WJ-MSC), the following improvements were observed within four months of treatment:

  • eGFR: 12.5 → 16.4 mL/min (Stage V → Stage IV)
  • Creatinine decreased from 4.7 to 3.9 mg/dL.
  • BUN: Improving trend
  • QOL Score (patient-reported symptoms and quality of life): Clear improvement

In this way, Stem Cell Therapy not only slows disease progression but also demonstrates concrete potential for restoring renal function.

Item

  • MSC demonstrated inhibition of renal inflammation, reduction in fibrosis, and restoration of function in animal experiments.
  • Preliminary clinical trials also confirmed the trend of eGFR improvement and safety.
  • End-stage renal disease cases have also demonstrated sufficient improvement to defer dialysis.
  • Stem cell therapy is receiving attention as a novel approach that could restore kidney function.

Is Stem Cell Therapy Safe? An Examination of Adverse Effects and Their Likelihood

Terapi Sel Punca, khususnya terapi menggunakan sel punca mesenchymal (MSC), telah dilaporkan dalam penelitian saat ini menunjukkan efek samping serius yang sedikit, sebagai metode pengobatan dengan keamanan tinggi.

Owing to the therapy's novelty, questions regarding its safety were frequently raised. Nevertheless, across the conducted research and clinical trials, no significant adverse events were reported in a substantial number of patients.

Virtually no adverse effects during administration.

For instance, in a clinical trial targeting diabetic nephropathy, allogeneic MSCs were administered once intravenously, and no serious treatment-related adverse events were observed. Notably, anti-HLA antibodies (markers of transplant rejection) were not detected in treated patients. This suggests the possibility that even if allogeneic cells are used, they do not elicit rejection responses and function safely within the body.

Similarly, in cases of end-stage renal failure utilizing Wharton's Jelly-derived MSCs, excellent safety was demonstrated. Following intravenous infusion, no fever or allergic reactions were observed, and the treatment was completed without complications.

Transient mild side effects may occur.

Generally, the reported side effects are the following mild ones:

  • Mild fever
  • Transient blood pressure fluctuations
  • Discomfort or pain at the injection site

All are transient, typically resolving naturally within a few days. Furthermore, due to MSCs' inherent immunomodulatory properties, serious hypersensitivity reactions, such as anaphylaxis, are rarely observed.

Areas Requiring Further Verification

However, given its relatively brief history as a therapy, the following points warrant careful future scrutiny:

  • Impact of repeated and sustained administration
  • Safety when applied to the elderly or even to patients with significant weight.
  • Identification of adverse effect risks through long-term monitoring.

Currently, the primary evaluation focuses on treatment methods that are safe and have minimal serious side effects. However, further data collection is anticipated in the future.

Item

  • In MSC treatment, virtually no serious side effects have been reported following administration.
  • Even from an allogeneic source, rejection reactions are infrequent, and immunological safety is high.
  • Should side effects occur, they are typically mild, such as a low-grade fever and injection site discomfort.
  • In the future, it will be necessary to further verify the safety of continuous administration, particularly in severe cases.

Conclusion: The Future Ushered In by Stem Cells

The prospect of avoiding dialysis holds immense significance for patients confronting the reality of renal failure.

Regenerative medicine, employing mesenchymal stem cells, particularly Wharton's Jelly-derived MSCs (WJ-MSCs), is gradually transforming that hope into a reality.

It is currently at a stage where it is provided by several medical facilities as part of private care. However, both domestic and international studies consistently report positive outcomes, including improvements in renal function, symptom alleviation, and a high safety profile.

Previously, when renal function had significantly declined, the only options were dialysis or transplantation. However, with the advent of Stem Cell Therapy, the option to "cure kidneys" has finally become practical.

Dengan kemajuan penelitian sel punca, frase "Jangan menyerah pada gagal ginjal" tidak lagi teori kosong, tetapi menjadi panduan praktis yang nyata untuk hidup di masa depan.
