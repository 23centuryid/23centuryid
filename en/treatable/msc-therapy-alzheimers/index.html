Status and Challenges of Alzheimer's Disease Treatment

Penyakit Alzheimer masih belum memiliki pengobatan fundamental, dan pendekatan baru yang dapat mengatasi berbagai aspek penyakit ini sangat dibutuhkan.

Current treatment focuses on inhibiting progression.

Alzheimer's disease is a progressive brain disorder characterized by memory impairment and a decline in cognitive function. It represents the most prevalent type of dementia, and its significance continues to grow with an aging population.

Current standard treatment employs cholinesterase inhibitors and NMDA receptor antagonists to balance neurotransmitters and slow the progression of symptoms.

However, these medications cannot halt disease progression or regenerate lost nerve cells.

The latest antibody drugs are also emerging, but limitations still exist.

Recently, antibodies targeting amyloid-beta and tau proteins—considered causative agents of Alzheimer's disease—have been developed, generating optimism. For example, in clinical trials, the anti-amyloid-beta antibody lecanemab was reported to reduce cognitive decline by approximately 27% over 18 months.

However, this latest treatment merely slows disease progression and has not achieved a complete cure. Furthermore, there is a risk of adverse effects, including cerebral hemorrhage and cerebral edema (ARIA), which necessitates cautious administration.

Complex Pathology Complicates Treatment

Alzheimer's disease manifests within the brain.

  • Amyloid-beta accumulation
  • Abnormal accumulation of tau protein
  • Chronic inflammation in the brain
  • Oxidative stress
  • Loss of synapses (connections between nerve cells)

These multiple pathological conditions occur concurrently.

Karena mekanisme penyakit yang kompleks ini, obat-obatan konvensional yang bekerja pada target tunggal tidak memberikan efektivitas yang cukup. Akibatnya, belum ada metode yang mapan untuk menghentikan penurunan fungsi kognitif. Ini menjadi tantangan besar bagi pasien dan keluarga mereka.

Given this background, a novel therapeutic approach that is broadly effective across various aspects of pathology is urgently needed.

Item

  • Alzheimer's disease is a representative progressive cognitive disorder.
  • Current medications only inhibit the progression of symptoms, rather than providing a fundamental cure.
  • The latest antibodies also offer a degree of efficacy, but do not lead to a cure.
  • Pathology is multifaceted, and single-target therapies have limitations.
  • There is significant optimism surrounding the new treatment strategy, which has the potential to address various aspects of the pathology.

Stem Cell Therapy Leading to 'Nerve Regeneration'

Alzheimer's disease is characterized by the loss of neurons. The endeavor to repair this damaged brain tissue has become a central pillar of regenerative therapy, specifically Stem Cell Therapy.

Stem cells are specialized cells characterized by their capacity for self-replication and differentiation into various cell types. These stem cells, extracted from patients or donors, are transplanted into the body to repair damaged tissues and restore function.

Keunggulan Terapi Sel Punca adalah mampu bekerja secara multidimensional terhadap patologi kompleks yang tidak dapat didekati oleh obat tunggal. Seperti "tim perbaikan infrastruktur kota yang rusak", terapi ini memberikan dukungan dari berbagai sisi: menenangkan peradangan saraf, melindungi sel, dan meregenerasi jaringan.

Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs) are a cell type receiving particular attention. They can be extracted from sources such as bone marrow, adipose tissue, and the umbilical cord. Their favorable balance between safety and therapeutic efficacy has led to a continuous expansion of research worldwide.

Why do umbilical cord-derived WJ-MSCs attract significant interest?

Mesenchymal stem cells (WJ-MSCs) derived from the gel-like tissue found within the umbilical cord—Wharton's jelly—have garnered significant attention as a "next-generation cell source" in regenerative therapies.

The reasons are divided into 4 main aspects:

  • Highly viable young cells, derived from newborns, exhibit minimal age-related degradation and possess exceptionally high proliferative capacity.
  • Its low immunogenicity and negligible MHC class II expression render it poorly detectable by the immune system, consequently leading to infrequent post-transplant rejection reactions.
  • Combining pluripotency and immunomodulatory activity, capable of differentiating into various cell types including bone, cartilage, adipose, and neural-like cells, while also possessing anti-inflammatory functions.
  • There are no ethical concerns, as naturally obtained umbilical cords from childbirth are utilized, posing minimal ethical implications.

Particularly for neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's, which cause central nervous system damage, the 'young cell' approach, offering multidimensional support, is paramount. In this context, WJ-MSCs can be likened to 'competent and dedicated young workers tasked with rebuilding the damaged cerebral 'city'.'

Given these characteristics, WJ-MSC is anticipated to emerge as a cell with innovative potential in the treatment of Alzheimer's disease and other neurodegenerative diseases.

  • Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) exhibit a superior balance of safety and efficacy.
  • WJ-MSC derived from the umbilical cord exhibit distinct advantages in cellular juvenescence, proliferative capacity, and immune tolerance.
  • Low immunogenicity results in rare post-transplant rejection reactions, rendering it suitable for transplantation.

How Does Stem Cell Therapy Work in Alzheimer's Disease?

Sel punca mesenkimal (MSC) bekerja secara multidimensional terhadap otak penyakit Alzheimer, mendorong pemulihan fungsi memori dan perbaikan kondisi patologi.

Stem Cell Therapy is not merely cell replacement.

Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs) demonstrate distinct advantages, particularly in addressing the complex pathology of Alzheimer's disease, by fulfilling multifaceted roles such as inflammation modulation, neurotrophic support, clearance of harmful substances, and neural circuit repair—all operating simultaneously.

We will now explain each mechanism in an easily comprehensible manner.

Mitigating Neuroinflammation

In Alzheimer's disease, immune cells (microglia) become dysregulated, causing chronic brain inflammation. Inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, TNF-α, etc.) are released in excess, leading to neuronal damage.

MSCs modulate this "uncontrolled inflammation." For example, in animal studies involving the intravenous administration of umbilical cord-derived MSCs, levels of the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 increased, whereas inflammatory cytokines significantly decreased.

This helps mitigate chronic inflammation in the brain and fosters an environment in which neuronal cells are protected.

Delivering Nutrients to Nerve Cells

MSCs also provide neurotrophic support. At the transplantation site, MSCs secrete various trophic factors, including brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), nerve growth factor (NGF), glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF), and insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1).

These factors support damaged neurons and promote the formation of new synapses (neural networks). Additionally, exosomes (small vesicles) released by MSCs also contain microRNAs and proteins with neuroprotective functions.

As nourishing rain descends upon parched earth, brain neurons are empowered to regenerate.

Brain waste clearance

Amyloid-beta plaque deposition and abnormal tau protein accumulation are hallmarks of Alzheimer's disease. These deleterious waste products impair neuronal function.

MSC not only suppresses inflammation but also supports the breakdown and clearance of amyloid-beta accumulated in the brain. In experiments, it was reported that amyloid-beta accumulation significantly decreased after MSC transplantation.

Functioning as a cellular clearance mechanism within the compromised cerebral environment, MSCs eliminate abnormal proteins and establish conditions conducive to neuronal functional recovery.

Reconnecting the Damaged Memory Circuit

Neurons in the brain are interconnected via synapses to support memory and cognitive function, but in Alzheimer's disease, this circuitry is damaged. MSC not only directly differentiates into neurons but also stimulates neural stem cells in the brain to promote neurogenesis and repair existing neural circuits.

In animal experiments, increased neurogenesis in the hippocampus (a brain region regulating memory) and enhanced synaptic density have been confirmed. Furthermore, MSCs also contribute to reducing oxidative stress and improving the overall brain environment.

In other words, MSC functions as a 'technician who rectifies faulty memory circuits'.

Item

  • MSC suppresses chronic inflammation in the brain and protects neurons.
  • It secretes nutritional factors to support cell survival and reconstruct neural tissue.
  • Promoting the clearance of abnormal amyloid-beta and tau proteins
  • Promotes neurogenesis and synaptic reconstruction.
  • Can comprehensively address multiple aspects of Alzheimer's disease pathology.

Recognized Beneficial Effects in Animal Models

Research utilizing animal models of Alzheimer's disease (genetically modified mice and rats) demonstrates that treatment with mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) has consistently reported restoration of cognitive function and amelioration of brain pathology.

For example, in mice that received intravenous administration of human umbilical cord-derived MSCs, spatial learning and memory functions significantly improved 4 weeks post-administration, and amyloid-beta accumulation in the brain was also reduced. Furthermore, inflammatory cytokines decreased and anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-10) increased, suggesting the resolution of chronic brain inflammation.

Furthermore, a technique has been developed to efficiently guide MSCs to the hippocampus (memory region) of mice by incorporating magnetic nanoparticles into the MSCs and guiding them with an external magnetic field. In these mice, improvements were observed in memory test performance and cognitive function, and neural functions critical for learning also improved.

In this animal experiment:

  • Decrease in inflammatory markers in the brain
  • Reduction of oxidative stress
  • Increased synaptic density
  • Reduction of amyloid-beta and tau proteins

Perlindungan dan perbaikan fungsi otak telah diakui dari berbagai sisi.
Seperti "tim regenerasi yang memperbaiki sebuah kota secara bersamaan", MSC merekonstruksi lingkungan otak dari berbagai sudut pandang.

Preliminary Clinical Trial Results in Patients

Building on the success of basic research, clinical trials in Alzheimer's disease patients have also commenced. These early-stage trials primarily aim to confirm safety, but promising results are already beginning to emerge.

For example, in a trial involving the transplantation of autologous stromal vascular fraction (SVF) into the brain ventricles, 8 out of 10 patients demonstrated stabilization or improvement in cognitive function, and in 3 patients, phosphorylated tau and amyloid-beta in cerebrospinal fluid were reduced.

Some cases have also demonstrated significant recovery of hippocampal volume.

Furthermore, in a clinical trial involving the intravenous administration of umbilical cord-derived MSCs conducted in Korea, safety was confirmed, and a trend towards the maintenance of activities of daily living (ADL) was observed. (This trial is progressing to Phase IIa)

Of particular interest is a placebo-controlled, double-blind, randomized trial with MSCs derived from Wharton's jelly (product name: Lomecel-B™) conducted in the United States.

In this trial, the patient group receiving MSC demonstrated:

  • Emphasis on the approximate 48% reduction in overall brain volume.
  • A pronounced reduction in hippocampal volume of approximately 62%.
  • Confirm reduction in inflammation with diffusion-weighted MRI.
  • Upward trend in cognitive function scores and quality of life indicators

These results demonstrate the potential for MSCs to halt cerebral atrophy and decelerate symptom progression.

Similar to the work of reinforcing a weakening bridge and preventing its complete collapse, stem cells play a role in mitigating the decline in brain function.

Is the safety of stem cell therapy assured? Verification of adverse effect risks.

Berdasarkan penelitian hingga saat ini, Terapi Sel Punca berasal dari Wharton's jelly (WJ-MSC) telah terbukti memiliki keamanan tinggi, memberikan jaminan besar bagi pasien.

In the context of Stem Cell Therapy, safety is the paramount concern. Particularly when utilizing allogeneic MSCs, concerns regarding rejection reactions and the potential for adverse effects are inherent.

However, extensive research indicates that treatment with MSCs, including WJ-MSCs, generally exhibits a high safety profile.

Confirmation of Safety in Initial Clinical Trials

In a Korean clinical trial involving the intravenous administration of umbilical cord-derived MSCs to Alzheimer's disease patients, the post-administration symptoms observed were:

  • Transient fever (9 of 9 cases)
  • Mild headaches (7 cases)
  • Nausea (5 cases)

All these symptoms spontaneously resolved within 36 hours. No long-term sequelae were observed.

In some patients, fever or nausea necessitated a one-day extension of hospitalization as a precaution. However, all cases resolved with appropriate management, and no serious toxicity (dose-limiting toxicity) was observed.

Furthermore, long-term monitoring up to 36 months did not reveal any new serious adverse effects. It is concluded that intravenous administration of umbilical cord-derived MSCs is feasible and safe.

Adverse Event Risk within the Overall Context of Stem Cell Therapy

Regarding Stem Cell Therapy generally, a 2022 meta-analysis evaluating its safety and efficacy across various neurodegenerative diseases indicates:

  • The incidence rate of medication-related adverse events is approximately 3%.

This is very low.

Overall, cell-based therapy is a safe and feasible therapeutic tool in the field of neurological diseases.

WJ-MSC Special Security Force

Notably, WJ-MSC offers additional safety assurance.

  • Having "difficult to detect" properties by the immune system, allowing it to be transplanted without immunosuppressants.
  • Adverse reactions are extremely rare.
  • The risk of tumor transformation is very low.

Pluripotent stem cells (e.g., embryonic stem cells [ESCs] and induced pluripotent stem cells [iPSCs]) carry a risk of tumor formation due to their incompletely differentiated state. In contrast, mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) are 'adult stem cells' that have achieved a certain level of differentiation. To date, in clinical applications, no post-transplantation tumor formation has been reported with MSCs.

On the contrary, MSC has:

  • Anticancer activity
  • Anti-fibrotic activity (the phenomenon of tissue hardening)

It is reported, thereby including safer therapeutic cells for use.

  • The incidence of serious adverse events across the entire therapeutic regimen is approximately 3%, a remarkably low figure.
  • WJ-MSC carries a very low risk of immune rejection and tumor transformation, providing peace of mind.
