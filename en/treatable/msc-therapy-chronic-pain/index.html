About Stem Cell Therapy for Chronic Pain

  • About Stem Cell Therapy for Chronic Pain

For individuals plagued by persistent pain that resists conventional treatments such as medication, nerve blocks, and rehabilitation, "Terapi Sel Punca" is emerging as a promising new hope for internal pain relief.

Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS), fibromyalgia, chronic back pain (particularly discogenic pain), and neuropathic pain are among many refractory conditions that cause chronic pain and lead to significant impairment in daily life.

For instance, even a light touch can induce excruciating pain, or pain severe enough to prevent sleep, profoundly altering not only the patient's life but also that of their family.

In many cases, traditional pain relievers or nerve blocks are not sufficiently effective. In such situations, "Stem Cell Therapy," which utilizes the body's own cells to reactivate its healing capabilities, emerges as a novel option to address the root cause of pain.

Why Stem Cells Are Effective for Pain: A Multifaceted Approach to the Ignition of Chronic Pain

Di balik nyeri kronis terdapat "percikan api" dari peradangan dan kerusakan yang terus-menerus. Sel punca (MSC) diharapkan dapat meredakan percikan api tersebut, memperbaiki jaringan, dan menyeimbangkan kegembiraan abnormal saraf, sehingga meredakan rasa sakit dari akarnya.

Chronic pain is more than just a "difficult-to-manage pain." Damaged tissues fail to heal adequately, inflammation is ongoing, and nerves remain hypersensitive; these issues are interconnected and self-perpetuating. Within this complex context, Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs) have garnered attention as a treatment that can facilitate recovery across various bodily systems and restore the body's intrinsic healing capabilities.

MSCs can be harvested from bone marrow, adipose tissue, umbilical cord, and other sources, and are widely applied in the field of regenerative medicine. For chronic pain, the following effects are anticipated.

Regenerating Tissue and Addressing Damage

Underlying persistent pain is a condition where tissues such as joints, intervertebral discs, and nerves remain damaged and unrepaired. Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs) accumulate at these damaged areas and release growth factors that promote cell proliferation and repair (e.g., HGF, TGF-β), thereby supporting tissue regeneration.

For example, when intervertebral discs degenerate and cause pain, it has been reported that MSC administration improves intervertebral disc thickness and flexibility, and reduces nerve compression.

Curbing runaway inflammation and preventing pain amplification

Another cause of chronic pain is persistent inflammation within the body. When inflammatory cytokines (e.g., TNF-α and IL-1β) are excessively secreted and continuously stimulate surrounding nerves and tissues, pain signals intensify, leading to a vicious cycle.

MSCs secrete anti-inflammatory cytokines (such as IL-10) and suppress pro-inflammatory mediators, thereby breaking this vicious cycle. To draw an analogy, MSCs act as an immune brake against the "fire" of inflammation.

Balancing Neural Pathways and Modulating Pain Signals

When the nerve itself is damaged, the sensitized nerve reacts to minimal stimuli with pain. This is referred to as "neuropathic pain," and conventional pain relievers are often ineffective.

MSCs secrete NGF (nerve growth factor) and BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor), among other factors, thereby promoting the repair of damaged nerves. It is anticipated that this will normalize aberrant pain signal transmission by "rewiring" damaged neural pathways.

Promoting Blood Flow and Preparing a Healing Environment

Inadequate blood flow is often the underlying issue behind poorly healing tissues. Specifically, in muscular and tendinous tissues, damage sustained in areas with limited vascularization leads to protracted recovery.

MSCs secrete VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor) and other factors, contributing to microvascular remodeling. Consequently, oxygen and nutrients are more readily delivered, improving the tissue's regenerative capacity.

When these effects are considered holistically, it can be stated that MSCs are not merely a symptomatic pain reliever, but rather a treatment aimed at restoring the physiological environment where pain originates to a normal state. MSCs effectively combine the roles of an 'emergency responder' and a 'repair specialist' within the body.

Item

  • Causes of chronic pain include inflammation, tissue damage, and excessive nerve sensitivity.
  • MSC possesses diverse functions, including inhibiting inflammation, facilitating tissue repair, and regulating neural function.
  • By increasing blood flow, the healing environment can also be enhanced.
  • By addressing the underlying causes of pain, the treatment approach aims to alleviate symptoms at their source.

Addressing the Persistence of Pain - A "Disease-Specific Approach" to Chronic Pain and Stem Cell Therapy

Untuk CRPS, fibromyalgia, nyeri punggung yang berasal dari diskus intervertebral, nyeri neuropati, dan kondisi nyeri kronis refrakter lainnya yang sulit ditingkatkan dengan pengobatan normal, tindakan multi-faset dari sel punca (MSC) mendapat perhatian sebagai terobosan baru.

The characteristics of chronic pain are not limited to its mere severity. Delayed healing of damaged tissue, persistent inflammation, and excessive nerve sensitivity are intricately linked, creating a vicious cycle that cannot be broken by medication or rehabilitation.

In such situations, mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) have garnered attention as a treatment method capable of addressing the 'root cause of chronic pain' through a multi-layered action of tissue regeneration, inflammation suppression, and neuroprotection, with ongoing research for each disease. Here, we introduce the therapeutic potential of MSCs for representative refractory chronic pain conditions.

CRPS: Modulating Inflammation and Nerves to Control Uncontrolled Pain

Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS) is a condition that develops following a minor injury or fracture, leading to severe pain, swelling, skin changes, and persistent joint stiffness in the affected area. A hallmark feature is allodynia, where even light touch elicits intense pain, along with an exaggerated sensitivity to stimuli such as heat, cold, and wind, which can significantly impair daily activities.

This pain is driven by a self-perpetuating pain cycle, characterized by persistent local inflammation and ongoing nerve hypersensitivity. Conventional analgesics or nerve blocks fail to provide adequate relief, and patients experience intractable pain despite therapeutic interventions.

Stem cells (MSCs) have the potential to apply a 'brake' to this runaway process. It is anticipated that the anti-inflammatory cytokines and neurotrophic factors released by MSCs will alleviate inflammation and attenuate neural excitation.

Notably, reports on intravenously administered allogeneic MSC therapy in CRPS patients have demonstrated a reduction in pain scores, improved joint mobility, and enhanced daily living activities. Furthermore, a clinical trial for MSC therapy in CRPS is currently underway at the Cleveland Clinic in the U.S., funded by the NIH (U.S. National Institutes of Health), fostering increasing optimism for establishing future treatment modalities.

Fibromyalgia: A Potential 'Restart' for an Overstimulated Brain

Fibromyalgia is a refractory disease characterized by persistent, diffuse pain, stiffness, fatigue, and insomnia throughout the body. It is more prevalent in women, and because its manifestations are not outwardly visible, it is sometimes misunderstood as a "lazy person's disease."

Underlying this is an exaggerated perception of pain from the brain and central nervous system, which constitutes the phenomenon of pain threshold amplification, causing stimuli that would normally go unnoticed to be perceived as intense pain.

Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs) have garnered attention due to their potential to reset dysfunctional neural responses. A 2023 animal study demonstrated that infusion of MSCs into fibromyalgia model mice resulted in a reduction in cerebral inflammatory cytokines and promoted neural cell protection and neogenesis.

It was also reported that the cellular inflammatory switch (NLRP3 inflammasome) was suppressed, and autophagy, which promotes neuronal self-repair, was activated.

Human clinical trials have recently commenced. However, concerning the normalization of the nervous system in instances where the pain threshold has been extremely lowered, MSC therapy could represent an entirely novel strategy for fibromyalgia. Moving forward, the potential for individualized therapies specifically targeting patients with severe symptoms is also anticipated to evolve.

Intervertebral Disc Back Pain: Regeneration of Spinal Discs for Pain Relief

Discogenic back pain is a condition characterized by the degeneration of the intervertebral discs, which serve as shock absorbers between the vertebrae, due to aging or excessive mechanical stress, resulting in nerve compression and subsequent chronic back pain.

Traditionally, if medication and rehabilitation fail to improve the condition, surgical intervention (fusion or intervertebral disc replacement) is considered. However, neither procedure restores the intervertebral disc itself. Consequently, these interventions often merely 'manage' rather than 'cure'.

This approach involves regenerating intervertebral discs using stem cells. When MSCs are injected into a degenerated intervertebral disc, it is hypothesized that cartilaginous tissue is regenerated, and pressure on the nerves is reduced.

An international collaborative trial reported that a single MSC injection resulted in a 50% reduction in pain for over half of the patients, with the effect persisting for more than 12 months. Additionally, in a separate trial, 67% of patients receiving MSC therapy demonstrated significant improvement in their Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) scores, which also led to reduced opioid medication use and the avoidance of surgical intervention.

Clinical research is currently underway in several countries, including Japan. For moderate to severe intervertebral disc degeneration, it is anticipated that MSC therapy will become a new option, bridging the 'gap' between conservative therapy and surgery.

Neuropathic Pain: The First Step to "Reconnecting" Damaged Nerves

Diabetic neuropathy, post-herpetic neuralgia, and chronic post-spinal cord injury pain—conditions where nerve damage itself is the etiology of "neuropathic pain"—are exceptionally challenging to manage. Conventional analgesics are ineffective, and given the premise that "nerves cannot regenerate," therapeutic options remain limited.

However, MSCs release neurotrophic factors to the injured nerve to promote regeneration and modulate the neural environment by alleviating inflammation around the nerve. Analogously, this is akin to meticulous repair work, reconnecting each frayed wire individually.

Reported trials involving patients with severe spinal cord injury demonstrated that subarachnoid administration of MSCs led to observed improvements in tactile sensation and bladder function, in addition to pain reduction. Furthermore, in models of diabetic neuropathy, it has also been confirmed that MSCs suppress inflammatory cell activity and promote myelin sheath regeneration, thereby contributing to the reduction of neuralgia.

Naturally, there are individual differences in the degree and causes of damage, so it cannot be stated that it is effective for everyone. However, at least as an initial step to reverse the ingrained belief that "regeneration is impossible," the availability of MSC therapy is highly significant.

  • Ongoing research into MSC therapy is addressing chronic pain conditions, including CRPS, fibromyalgia, discogenic back pain, and neuropathic pain.
  • In every disease, various measures such as inflammation suppression, neural repair, and tissue regeneration are indicated.
  • Research has progressed from animal studies to clinical research, and a transition to healthcare insurance and the standardization of care is anticipated in the future.
  • For chronic pain, often described as 'incurable', MSC offers significant hope and promise as a fundamental approach.

Is Stem Cell Therapy Safe? – Side Effects and Future Considerations

Terapi Sel Punca (MSC) telah menunjukkan dalam banyak penelitian klinis bahwa efek samping serius hampir tidak dilaporkan, dan dievaluasi sebagai perawatan dengan keamanan tinggi. Namun, kedokteran regeneratif adalah kedokteran dalam perjalanan evolusi, dan syarat utama adalah bahwa itu disediakan di bawah manajemen yang tepat.

While some may perceive regenerative medicine as an unconventional treatment, therapies utilizing mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) have found widespread clinical application globally, and a degree of safety has been confirmed.

Risk of Rejection and Malignancy is Very Low

A key reason MSCs have garnered attention is their low immunogenicity. If the cells are autologous (the patient's own), rejection is not expected to occur in principle; however, even when allogeneic cells (from another individual) are used, MSCs are less susceptible to immune system attack, thereby confirming a high safety profile.

Regarding the risk of malignancy, MSCs are classified as 'adult stem cells,' and unlike iPS cells, the risk of malignancy due to cellular immaturity is very low. In previous clinical studies, no tumor formation directly caused by MSCs has been reported.

There are individual differences in treatment effects.

The effects of MSC therapy are dependent on the route of administration (transfusion, local injection), the type of cells used, the stage of disease progression, and other factors. Consequently, the therapeutic effects do not manifest uniformly across all individuals, necessitating a precise evaluation tailored to each patient's specific status.

Furthermore, the dose and administration interval, as well as the long-term persistence of effects, require further elucidation in future clinical studies.
