Parkinson's Disease - Current State and Challenges

Parkinson's disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disease characterized by the gradual loss of dopamine-producing neurons in the brain. Approximately 1% of individuals aged 65 years and older are reported to be affected by this condition.

Its primary symptoms include movement disorders such as tremor, muscle rigidity, and slowness of movement (bradykinesia). As the condition progresses, non-motor symptoms also emerge, such as depression, anxiety, cognitive impairment, and autonomic dysfunction, which significantly impact quality of life.

Current treatments primarily focus on dopamine-replacement pharmacotherapy and deep brain stimulation (DBS). While these provide transient symptomatic relief, drug efficacy diminishes over time, and new adverse effects and challenges arise.

Furthermore, no existing treatment can halt the neurodegeneration itself, and non-motor symptoms are also not adequately addressed. There is an urgent need for novel therapeutic approaches that not only suppress symptoms but also tackle the underlying disease progression.

A New Approach to the Treatment of Parkinson's Disease through Regenerative Therapy

Melengkapi dan memperbaiki sel saraf yang hilang dalam penyakit Parkinson dengan kekuatan sel punca—"pengobatan yang sebelumnya mustahil untuk meregenerasi saraf itu sendiri" telah mengambil langkah besar menuju kenyataan melalui terapi regeneratif.

Intervention Approach at the Cellular Level

In Parkinson's disease, dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra region of the brain degenerate and are lost. This cellular loss is the underlying cause that precipitates motor impairments and various non-motor symptoms.

Symptomatic treatment provides only temporary relief through dopaminergic medications (e.g., levodopa) and cannot restore lost neurons.

In response to this challenge, regenerative therapy utilizes stem cells, which has garnered significant attention in recent years.

Advances in Stem Cell Therapy in Parkinson's Disease

In the 1980s, experiments were conducted to transplant dopamine-producing neurons derived from fetal brain into patients. Although some effects were reported,

  • Ethical concerns (societal anxieties regarding the use of fetal networks)
  • Low survival rate of transplanted cells
  • Immune rejection

Serious problems of this nature exist, and have never seen widespread implementation.

Currently, research is evolving towards the utilization of safer and more stable cell sources, with mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), and neural stem cells garnering significant attention.

The Promise of Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSC)

MSC is a stem cell that can be relatively easily extracted from bone marrow, adipose tissue, and umbilical cord, and

  • Possessing high proliferative capacity
  • Inducible to differentiate into neurons
  • Secretes cytokines that protect and repair damaged tissue.

Possesses such characteristics.

Khususnya dalam penyakit Parkinson, kemungkinan melengkapi neuron dopamin yang hilang dan meningkatkan lingkungan otak itu sendiri telah ditunjukkan, dan dalam beberapa tahun terakhir telah menjadi salah satu pendekatan yang paling menarik perhatian dalam bidang terapi regeneratif.

Key Points

  • The loss of dopamine neurons is the underlying cause of Parkinson's disease.
  • Traditional medicine cannot regenerate nerves itself.
  • Stem Cell Therapy enables a fundamental approach to the replacement of lost nerve cells.
  • Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs) are potent therapeutic candidates, combining differentiation potential and protective effects.

How Stem Cell Therapy Works in Parkinson's Disease

Terapi Sel Punca (terutama sel punca mesenkimal = MSC) mendekati secara multi-fasia hilangnya sel saraf yang terlibat dalam perkembangan penyakit Parkinson dan deteriorasi lingkungan otak, dengan tujuan perbaikan fungsi mendasar.

Replenishing Lost Dopamine Neurons

It has been demonstrated that MSCs possess the ability to differentiate into dopaminergic neurons under appropriate conditions.

In experiments, when MSC-derived cells were transplanted into the brain, they extended axons and formed synapses, and are believed to contribute to the reconstruction of disrupted neural circuits by secreting dopamine.

Leveraging MSCs, the potential for not only neuroprotection but also the replacement of lost neurons has become evident.

Protecting Damaged Nerves (Neuroprotection)

MSCs, also referred to as "living cell factories," secrete numerous beneficial cytokines and growth factors into their surroundings (e.g., BDNF, NGF, IGF-1, etc.). In this manner,

  • Promoting the survival of damaged nerve cells.
  • Promoting nerve repair and regeneration
  • Soothing neural inflammation

It is expected to demonstrate such neuroprotective effects and halt the progression of Parkinson's disease. In animal experiments utilizing umbilical cord-derived MSCs, an increase in intra-cerebral IGF-1 was also accompanied by improved motor function and increased memory impairment.

Adjusting the Brain's Environment Itself

Exosomes secreted by Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs) contain substances with pro-angiogenic, antioxidant, and anti-apoptotic properties. These substances traverse the blood-brain barrier to access the brain.

  • Formation of new capillaries
  • Regeneration of the microenvironment (niche) around nerve cells
  • Inhibition of dopaminergic neuron death and restoration of dopamine concentration

Demonstrated in animal experiments, Stem Cell Therapy is not merely "cell replacement," but also an approach to reshape the brain's landscape.

Key Points

  • MSC differentiate into dopaminergic neurons and reconstruct lost neural tissue.
  • Secreting abundant growth factors and promoting neuronal protection and repair.
  • Soothing chronic inflammation and enhancing the entire brain environment (niche) to a state conducive to neural regeneration.
  • A multi-faceted approach for the restoration of underlying function in Parkinson's disease.

A Perspective on Stem Cell Therapy for Improving Parkinson's Disease Symptoms

Terapi Sel Punca untuk penyakit Parkinson telah melaporkan banyak hasil yang menjanjikan dari eksperimen hewan hingga uji klinis, menunjukkan perbaikan gejala.

Effects Demonstrated in Preclinical Research

During the basic research phase (preclinical studies), MSC transplantation in animal models of Parkinson's disease (rats and mice) has demonstrated significant improvements in motor function and pathological findings.

  • In models administered umbilical cord-derived MSCs, reduced motor impairment, suppressed neuroinflammation, and enhanced dopaminergic neuron survival were reported.
  • Bone marrow-derived MSC transplantation also reduced dopaminergic neuron death in the substantia nigra, and its neuroprotective effect against neuronal damage was confirmed.
  • Wharton's Jelly-derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs) have been reported to demonstrate not only recovery of motor symptoms but also improvement in non-motor symptoms, such as cognitive impairment and pain hypersensitivity.

Furthermore, research reports that umbilical cord MSC transplantation restores BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor) and NGF (Nerve Growth Factor) in the brain, and improvements in hippocampal synaptic plasticity (learning and memory function) are reported.

Expected Outcomes in Clinical Research

Human clinical trials have also demonstrated positive results, although on a smaller scale.

  • A recent systematic review, analyzing data from 9 trials comprising 129 Parkinson's disease patients, found that the stem cell transplantation group demonstrated significant symptomatic improvement compared with the conventional treatment group alone.
  • UPDRS (Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale) scores significantly decreased in the transplant group, and the effect was reported to persist for at least 1 year.
  • Specifically, umbilical cord MSCs are anticipated to yield superior outcomes due to their youthful cellular state and high differentiation capacity.

Additionally, in one trial involving the transplantation of bone marrow-derived MSCs into the brain, with up to 3 years of follow-up, UPDRS scores improved by approximately 23% in the off-medication state and by approximately 38% in the on-medication state, and successful reduction of oral medication doses was reported.

Future Hopes and Challenges

Certainly, current clinical data are limited, and case numbers are not substantial. Nevertheless, it has progressed beyond the stage where its effectiveness was deemed unclear, and stronger evidence is expected to be gathered from ongoing clinical trials worldwide.

Pada saat ini juga, jelas bahwa Terapi Sel Punca untuk penyakit Parkinson telah menjadi pilihan pengobatan baru dengan kemungkinan perbaikan gejala yang jelas.

Key Points

  • Preclinical studies have confirmed the protective effects on motor and neurological function.
  • In clinical trials, improvements in UPDRS scores and a trend of symptom mitigation were observed.
  • Umbilical cord MSCs, in particular, hold great promise due to their youthful state and high differentiation capacity.
  • It is anticipated that effectiveness will be more firmly established through future large-scale trials.

Safety and Risk Assessment of Stem Cell Therapy for Parkinson's Disease

Terapi Sel Punca (terapi MSC) telah menunjukkan keamanan tinggi dalam penelitian sebelumnya, dan secara bertahap menjadi opsi pengobatan yang dapat dipilih dengan percaya diri oleh pasien Parkinson.

Research Report on the Safety of MSC Therapy

Primarily, therapy involving allogeneic mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) minimizes immune rejection reactions, and serious adverse events are rarely reported.

  • In a clinical trial conducted in patients with Parkinson's Disease, following intravenous injection of MSCs from different donors, no donor-specific immune response (rejection) was detected, and high safety and tolerability were demonstrated.
  • In a pilot study of autologous MSC transplantation into the brain, with a maximum follow-up of 36 months, no tumor formation or serious adverse events were observed.

In this trial, positive biological changes were observed following cell administration, such as a decrease in inflammatory cytokines and an increase in neurotrophic factors (BDNF, IGF-1, etc.), as well as increased cerebral blood flow.

Common Side Effects and Risk Management

Efek samping yang dilaporkan dalam terapi MSC terbatas pada rasa sakit sementara di lokasi infus, demam, dan sakit kepala ringan. Dibandingkan dengan pengobatan standar, pengobatan ini dievaluasi sebagai pengobatan yang sangat aman.

Specifically, MSCs derived from Wharton's Jelly are sourced from newborns, exhibiting a high cell initialization rate, and are considered to have a low risk of genetic damage and aging.

The Importance of Healthcare Systems and Quality Management

As Stem Cell Therapy requires advanced medical technology,

  • Rigorous infection control during the cell culture process.
  • Selection of the appropriate cell count and administration route
  • Manufacturing and management in accordance with GMP standards

Comprehensive quality management and professional techniques of this nature are imperative. Currently, these are safely implemented in compliant facilities, with further optimization anticipated in the future.

Key Points

  • In previous clinical trials, several serious adverse events associated with MSC treatment were reported.
  • A low risk of immune rejection and high tolerance have been confirmed following transplantation.
  • Wharton's Jelly MSCs, in particular, are highly likely to demonstrate superior safety.
  • The comprehensive implementation of infection management and manufacturing quality enables safer treatment delivery.
  • Overall, MSC Stem Cell Therapy is a promising option with a high safety profile.
