Stem Cell Therapy for Traumatic Brain Injury

Preliminary Introduction

Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) is a medical condition resulting from damage to brain tissue due to a forceful impact to the head from traffic accidents, falls, or similar incidents. In severe cases, it can lead to serious symptoms such as loss of consciousness, memory loss, hemiplegia, or speech impairment, often resulting in permanent, lifelong disabilities.

Currently, TBI patients and their families engage in acute management during the initial phase and functional recovery through rehabilitation. However, they are confronted with the challenge that "damaged brain cells do not regenerate." In reality, there is no scientific evidence that dead or damaged brain cells regenerate as new cells. While the remaining brain regions make significant efforts to compensate for lost functions, issues often persist in motor skills, cognitive abilities, and speech, as well as personality changes and symptoms of depression.

This type of disability significantly reduces the quality of life (QOL) and imposes a burden on the patient and their family.

Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) and Current Treatment Challenges

The current standard of care for TBI primarily centers on acute phase emergency management and subsequent rehabilitation. Specifically, immediately post-injury, surgical intervention and intensive care, including hematoma evacuation and intracranial pressure control, are provided. Subsequently, physical and occupational therapies are initiated to optimize and enhance residual function.

However, once brain tissue sustains serious damage, it is inherently challenging to fully restore it to normal function solely through medication or or rehabilitation. The brain is often considered a "difficult-to-heal organ," and to date, no medical treatment has successfully regenerated nerve cells themselves in the damaged area.

Consequently, despite many patients achieving some improvement through rehabilitation, they continue to live with various forms of long-term disability, such as paralysis or memory impairment. The current therapeutic challenge lies in how to regenerate these lost brain cells. Patients and their families are keenly seeking novel treatments that offer fundamental brain repair.

Emerging Possibilities of Stem Cell Therapy and Regenerative Medicine for Traumatic Brain Injury

In recent years, stem cell therapy, leveraging regenerative medicine principles, has garnered significant attention. This cutting-edge medical treatment utilizes naturally occurring stem cells within our bodies – cells capable of differentiating into various cell types – to promote the repair and regeneration of damaged tissues. Even in cases of previously intractable brain damage, stem cells are expected to unleash the body's inherent regenerative capacity, offering the potential to restore lost brain function.

Currently, research and clinical trials on stem cell therapy for TBI are underway worldwide, and new regenerative medicine products are being continuously developed. For instance, efforts are ongoing to transplant stem cells into damaged brain tissue and restore functions previously assumed by different brain regions by "regenerating the lost cells themselves." Through such novel possibilities in regenerative medicine, it is hoped that in the future, severe post-traumatic disabilities can be avoided.

Mechanism of Action of Stem Cell Therapy for Traumatic Brain Injury

Tissue Regeneration at the Site of Damage

Transplanted stem cells function as a "brain repair mechanism." These cells differentiate into neural or vascular cells as required, generating new cells and filling areas of damaged tissue. To illustrate, consider a construction team repairing potholes in a road. Stem cells act as the workers in this repair process, incrementally producing new "asphalt" (cells) and seamlessly resurfacing the "potholed road" (brain circuits). This process facilitates the repair of brain tissue, which is typically considered to have limited regenerative capacity, potentially leading to functional recovery.

Stem Cell Therapy for Traumatic Brain Injury

Activating surrounding cells

Stem cells not only differentiate into new cells but also act as a conductor, orchestrating surrounding cells with signals to "collaborate for repair!" Indeed, it is reported that substances such as cytokines and growth factors released from stem cells promote the survival of adjacent neural cells and activate the function of endogenous neural stem cells within the brain. Metaphorically, stem cells become the "leader of a reconstruction team dispatched to a damaged city," engaging with the remaining cells (the "inhabitants") to stimulate the overall recovery of the city (the enhancement of overall brain function).

Clinical Trial Achievements for Chronic TBI Patients (SB623)

An international clinical trial using the stem cell therapy "SB623," developed by the Japanese company Sanbio, demonstrated significant improvement in the motor function of chronic traumatic brain injury (TBI) patients who were 6 months to several years post-injury. Specifically, 39.1% of the group receiving intracerebral stem cell transplantation achieved a large, clinically meaningful improvement in their Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale (FMMS) scores for paralyzed upper and lower extremities (+10 points or more), significantly exceeding the 6.7% observed in the control group.

In this experiment, the observed improvement was not transient; significant recovery was reportedly maintained even at the 1-year follow-up.
