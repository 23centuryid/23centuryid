Sjögren's Syndrome is an autoimmune disease in which the body's immune system mistakenly attacks the lacrimal and salivary glands, leading to severe dry eyes and dry mouth.

Due to the difficulty in producing tears and saliva, patients experience not only oral itching and discomfort but also decreased vision and an increased risk of dental caries and infections. As the disease progresses, patients also develop systemic symptoms such as joint pain and extreme fatigue, which significantly diminishes their quality of life.

Currently, no treatments fundamentally cure immune disorders, and current therapy focuses on symptomatic management. For example, Sjögren's patients receive the following:

  • Dry eye strategy: Artificial tears and lubricating eye ointment
  • Dry mouth strategies: Frequent water intake, salivary gland massage, artificial saliva sprays/gels, oral medications to promote salivary secretion (e.g., pilocarpine agents).
  • Systemic therapy for symptoms includes: Anti-inflammatory drugs such as antirheumatic agents, steroids, immunosuppressants, and biological agents (e.g., rituximab), depending on the symptoms and severity.

Nevertheless, these treatments present a number of limitations.

Artificial tears and saliva only provide temporary hydration and are ineffective against the underlying immune disorder, necessitating repeated administration. Salivary secretagogues also do not fully ameliorate symptoms and cannot halt the progression of the disease itself.

Immunosuppressants and steroids can help alleviate systemic inflammation. However, long-term use raises concerns regarding an increased risk of adverse effects such as infection, osteoporosis, and diabetes. Furthermore, these medications often cannot fully improve the symptoms of dryness itself, and many patients report dissatisfaction with their effects.

Thus, there remains a substantial unmet medical need for the definitive treatment of Sjögren's Syndrome.

Sjögren's Syndrome and Stem Cell Therapy: New Possibilities for Regenerative Medicine

In light of the current situation, novel approaches from the field of regenerative medicine have recently garnered significant attention. A prominent example is stem cell therapy.

Stem cells are specialized cells that possess the ability to differentiate into various cell types and the capacity to repair damaged tissues. Among these, mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) are being investigated as promising cells for the treatment of autoimmune diseases.

MSCs can be harvested from various sources such as bone marrow, adipose tissue, and umbilical cord, and can be cultured and expanded for use in patient treatment. Indeed, therapeutic research utilizing MSCs for chronic diseases, including Sjögren's Syndrome, is being conducted worldwide, with indications that "dryness can be alleviated."

Current experimental and clinical research indicates that stem cell therapy restores lacrimal and salivary secretory function in Sjögren's Syndrome animal models and patients, and reduces inflammatory cell infiltration in the salivary glands.

This is a unique benefit of regenerative medicine that conventional symptomatic therapies lack.

Advantages of Wharton's Jelly-derived stem cells (WJ-MSC):

There are several types of stem cells depending on their source of derivation, but recent particular attention has been given to mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) derived from Wharton's Jelly, which is contained within the umbilical cord.

Wharton's Jelly-derived stem cells are immature cells that can be collected non-invasively from umbilical cord tissue obtained at birth, and are reported to possess high proliferative capacity and immunomodulatory capabilities.

Derived from neonatal cells, these are "young and healthy cells," and even when donor cells are directly transferred to other patients, rejection reactions are rarely observed. This unique immunological property is attributed to protecting the fetus from maternal immunity, and cells derived from the umbilical cord and placenta are considered to contain numerous immunosuppressive substances.

In stem cell therapy research, while stem cells derived from bone marrow and adipose tissue are also utilized, umbilical cord-derived stem cells offer significant advantages concerning the ease of cell collection and minimal donor burden.

In this way, stem cell therapy is anticipated as a novel treatment strategy offering hope to patients with Sjögren's Syndrome. While currently still in the research phase, its gradually elucidating effects and mechanisms will be examined in further detail below.

Mechanism of Action of Stem Cell Therapy for Sjögren's Syndrome

Why is Sjögren's Syndrome exacerbated by stem cells?

The key lies in the immunomodulatory capabilities and tissue regeneration-promoting effects of mesenchymal stem cells. In Sjögren's Syndrome, the immune system becomes dysregulated and aberrantly attacks its own lacrimal and salivary glands.

Stem cell therapy functions to modulate the dysregulated immune system. Specifically, the diverse substances secreted by stem cells (cytokines and growth factors, collectively referred to as the "secretome") act on immune cells, mitigating excessive inflammatory responses.

It has been confirmed that anti-inflammatory cytokines released by stem cells reduce the production of pro-inflammatory mediators (such as TNF-α and IL-17) and increase anti-inflammatory mediators (such as IL-10).

In this manner, the autoimmune reactions that have damaged the lacrimal and salivary glands are attenuated.

Additionally, mesenchymal stem cells also possess the ability to aid in the repair of damaged tissues. These stem cells accumulate in damaged lacrimal and salivary gland tissues, where they actively promote tissue regeneration.

Stem cells inherently possess the capacity to differentiate and generate new cells. However, their more significant contribution lies in their supportive effect on surrounding tissues, mediated by the secretion of growth factors and angiogenic factors. For instance, much like providing fertilizer and water to a drought-stricken field, stem cells deliver essential nutrients and signals to compromised areas, thereby stimulating the intrinsic self-repair mechanisms of the cells themselves.

In this manner, the restoration of function in damaged glandular tissue (restoring the ability to produce tears and saliva) is anticipated. Furthermore, MSCs also possess the ability to suppress tissue fibrosis (hardening) and promote new capillary formation, thereby improving the glandular tissue environment and facilitating moisture production.

Furthermore, mesenchymal stem cells also enhance immune regulatory cells that are not readily achievable with other treatments. For example, studies have reported that MSC administration increases immune-suppressive regulatory T cells (Treg) and reduces pro-inflammatory Th17 cells. In this way, by shifting the immune balance towards normalization, the effect of breaking the autoimmune vicious cycle can be anticipated.

Stem cell therapy not only provides temporary symptomatic relief but operates on two fronts: by "calming the immune frenzy" and simultaneously "promoting the regeneration of damaged tissue," thereby being considered a fundamental correction for Sjögren's Syndrome.

Evidence of Stem Cell Therapy Effectiveness for Sjögren's Syndrome

What is the efficacy of stem cell therapy? Small-scale clinical studies and clinical trial reports have been conducted worldwide, with the majority reporting promising results.

Restoration of secretory function

It has been confirmed that stem cell therapy enhances the volume of secreted tears and saliva. Specifically, in a clinical trial for Sjögren's Syndrome conducted in China, the secretory function of the salivary and lacrimal glands significantly improved at 3 months post-treatment compared to the placebo group. In another study involving intravenous infusion of Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs), many patients experienced an increase in saliva production. Furthermore, regarding dry eyes, it was reported that tear secretion tended to increase, leading to a reduction in both dry eye and dry mouth symptoms.

Alleviate symptoms

Patients receiving stem cell therapy reported improvement in subjective symptoms, including systemic fatigue and joint pain, as well as dryness.

The patient symptom evaluation score (ESS-PRI index) significantly increased in the treatment group compared to the placebo group, indicating an improvement in quality of life, including reduced pain and fatigue.

Decrease in disease activity

Improvements in indicators of Sjögren's disease activity (ESSDAI score) were also reported. For instance, in one clinical study, following MSC treatment, disease activity scores significantly decreased. Three months post-treatment, 46% of patients showed a 30% or greater improvement in their disease. This figure rose to 71% at 6 months and 83% at 1 year.

A trend of increasing effectiveness over time was also confirmed, with significant expectations placed on the sustained efficacy of stem cell therapy. Indeed, in a separate trial conducted in China, ESSDAI scores significantly decreased for up to 6 months post-treatment. Furthermore, indicators reflecting systemic inflammation (ESR, erythrocyte sedimentation rate) and patient functional evaluations (ESSPRI) also showed improvement, demonstrating that stem cell therapy suppresses disease activity.

Correction of immune disorders

Data also indicates that stem cell therapy reduces autoantibodies and inflammatory substances characteristic of Sjögren's Syndrome.

For instance, it has been reported that blood concentrations of anti-SSA/Ro and anti-SSB/La autoantibodies decreased in some patients undergoing treatment, indicating an improvement in indicators of immune abnormalities. Furthermore, in previous clinical trials, levels of excessive immunoglobulins such as IgG and IgM were reduced in the stem cell therapy group. This indicates that excessive autoimmune reactions in the body have subsided, providing evidence that stem cell therapy improves the pathophysiology of Sjögren's Syndrome itself. Additionally, in tissue biopsy findings, following stem cell therapy, lymphocyte infiltration (inflammatory cell infiltration) in the salivary glands was significantly reduced.

Thus, based on multi-faceted indicators, MSC stem cell therapy demonstrates promising effects for Sjögren's Syndrome. While much of this evidence remains in the research phase, it continues to accumulate globally. Notably, the significant improvement in symptoms and test data observed in treatments with high safety profiles is highly encouraging for patients.

Stem cell therapy is beginning to show promise in delivering fundamental effects that symptomatic therapy alone cannot achieve, particularly for the persistent dryness symptoms, a long-standing challenge in Sjögren's Syndrome.

Safety of Stem Cell Therapy for Sjögren's Syndrome and Side Effects

In general, adverse effects in stem cell therapy are mild and transient.

Following intravenous infusion, it is rare to observe mild, transient fever, headache, or fatigue; however, these symptoms typically resolve rapidly within a short period and leave no long-term effects.

Clinical trial reports also indicated that among 64 patients who received the treatment, only two experienced transient pruritus, and no other adverse events were observed. Consequently, stem cell therapy is regarded as a treatment with an exceptionally high safety profile.

Furthermore, stem cells primarily modulate the immune system, unlike steroid medications that induce profound immunosuppression. Consequently, an increased risk of infection is not anticipated. In fact, there have been no reports of severe infections attributable to stem cell therapy. Conversely, there are instances where MSCs have been applied in difficult-to-treat infection cases (e.g., MSC administration for severe GVHD alongside other treatments).

Additionally, stem cell therapy does not require high-dose chemotherapy or prior general anesthesia, unlike bone marrow transplantation, thereby reducing the physical burden on patients. Therefore, stem cell therapy for Sjögren's syndrome can be considered a treatment option with a high safety profile and minimal concerns regarding adverse effects.

In conclusion, the favorable safety profile observed in research thus far is a key reason for optimism regarding stem cell therapy. Current studies report few serious adverse events. Moreover, even in over 400 patients with autoimmune diseases who received mesenchymal stem cell infusions, the incidence of serious treatment-related adverse events was very low, demonstrating a high safety profile.

Conclusion

For Sjögren's Syndrome patients, "a life free from dryness" is a profound aspiration. Advances in stem cell therapy and regenerative medicine are beginning to offer a glimmer of hope for what was previously an elusive goal.

Stem cell therapy is a a novel approach that aims for fundamental improvement in Sjögren's Syndrome by calming the overactive immune system and repairing damaged tissues.

Results from ongoing research are progressively revealing significant improvements in symptoms and a high safety profile, and accumulating evidence continues to offer hope to patients. It is important to note that stem cell therapy has not yet been established as a standard treatment, and further research and verification are ongoing. However, for patients who are anxious that current treatments offer no further benefit, stem cell therapy may represent a new option.

Given its demonstrated effectiveness and safety, it is also plausible that in the future, stem cell therapy will be widely adopted as a standard treatment for Sjögren's Syndrome. Crucially, the underlying principle of stem cell therapy is to unlock the patient's own intrinsic healing capabilities, which contributes to its appeal as a well-tolerated therapeutic approach.

Ending days disrupted by debilitating dryness, and restoring essential moisture and comfort — stem cell therapy is a novel, cutting-edge treatment offering peace of mind and renewed hope to patients battling Sjögren's Syndrome.
