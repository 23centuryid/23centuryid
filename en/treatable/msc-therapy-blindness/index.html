Blindness no longer signifies an irreversible state. With advancements in regenerative medicine, individuals who have lost their sight now have the potential to perceive light once more.

Stem cells derived from a tissue called Wharton's Jelly, found within the umbilical cord of infants, offer hope for restoring some lost visual function or halting disease progression.

For example, several patients undergoing therapy reported improved color differentiation and an enhanced ability to perceive approaching vehicles from a distance.

Furthermore, the speed of reading text on smartphones improves, and morning vision becomes clearer, with the effects lasting several hours.

It is akin to a closed door gradually opening, allowing light to begin to filter through.

Despite being a minor change, for this patient, it signifies a crucial first step towards emerging from a state of complete darkness, profoundly impacting their daily life and emotional well-being.

Regenerative Potential of the Umbilical Cord – What are Wharton's Jelly Stem Cells?

Mesenchymal Stem Cells from Wharton's Jelly (WJ-MSC) are "cells of hope" in regenerative medicine that combine extraordinary repair capabilities with high safety.

Mesenchymal Stem Cells from Wharton's Jelly (WJ-MSC) are a type of specialized stem cell derived from a gelatinous tissue known as Wharton's Jelly, found within the umbilical cord of a newborn. Post-natally, the umbilical cord is typically discarded; however, it harbors a rich source of stem cells with the capacity to repair and regenerate various body tissues.

WJ-MSCs are a type of mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), characterized by their versatility in contributing to the repair of various tissues, including neural, vascular, bone, and cartilage. Moreover, utilizing cell culture technology, they can be expanded in large quantities, thus ensuring an adequate supply for therapeutic applications.

It is important to note that this cell possesses "immunological privilege," meaning it does not readily elicit rejection reactions even when allogeneically transplanted. This is because the surface of WJ-MSCs expresses very low levels of immunogenic marker molecules (such as MHC class II), which supports a high safety profile in clinical applications.

Furthermore, it has been demonstrated that the risk of tumor (cancer) transformation from WJ-MSCs is very low, with no reported cases of patients experiencing abnormal growths or lumps post-therapy. Ethically, there are no concerns as the cells are derived from umbilical cords that would otherwise be discarded, and this cell source is considered safe for use.

Thus, WJ-MSC, which integrates safety, efficacy, and ethical considerations, is currently garnering significant attention in regenerative medicine clinics worldwide and is regarded as a crucial 'key' for restoring vision in challenging conditions such as blindness.

Key Point

  • WJ-MSC is a stem cell derived from infant umbilical cord with high regenerative capabilities.
  • Exhibits 'immunological privilege,' rendering it less prone to rejection even from an allogeneic donor.
  • The risk of tumor transformation is extremely low, and its safety profile is excellent.
  • This cell source is ethically unobjectionable, as it is derived from umbilical cords that would otherwise be discarded.
  • Gained global attention in regenerative medicine as the "key" to restoring vision.

Mechanism of Stem Cell-Mediated Ocular Repair

WJ-MSC works as a "supporter" that protects the damaged retina, reactivates it, and awakens the ability to perceive light again.

Blindness is predominantly caused by damage or degeneration of retinal nerve cells (photoreceptor cells or optic nerve fibers) and retinal pigment epithelial cells, leading to impaired function. Herein, Wharton's Jelly-derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells (WJ-MSCs) play a crucial role. WJ-MSCs support injured ocular cells and promote the healing process towards functional restoration.

The properties demonstrated by WJ-MSC can be divided into two broad categories.

Cellular Repair Support (Paracrine Effect)

WJ-MSCs secrete a rich array of cytokines and growth factors to stimulate surrounding cells. These growth factors act directly on the retina to:

  • Alleviating inflammation
  • Preventing cell death (apoptosis)
  • Reducing oxidative stress (cellular rust)
  • Increasing blood flow and nutrient supply

Supporting the creation of an optimal environment for regeneration.

Additionally, WJ-MSCs possess a unique function: they transfer their mitochondria (the cell's powerhouses) to damaged cells to provide supplementary energy. This mechanism enables weakened photoreceptor cells to regain activity and enhances the function of surviving cells.

Differentiation into Ocular Cells (Differentiation Capacity)

Furthermore, WJ-MSCs have been reported to possess properties similar to photoreceptor cells or retinal pigment epithelial cells in vivo. In animal experiments, injected MSCs demonstrated light-sensing capabilities after engraftment into the retina, and hold significant promise for human application.

At this current stage, WJ-MSCs are considered to "assist surrounding cells in becoming stronger and stimulate self-repair" rather than "directly replacing lost cells." However, even this mechanism alone has brought hope for the restoration of visual function, a previously unattainable outcome through conventional methods, and represents a significant force in unlocking the future of regenerative medicine.

Key Point

  • WJ-MSC secrete cytokines and growth factors to suppress retinal inflammation and promote repair.
  • Transferring mitochondria to damaged cells to provide additional energy.
  • Certain WJ-MSCs may possess properties similar to photoreceptor cells or retinal cells.
  • The primary effect is "helping surrounding cells to become stronger and triggering self-repair" rather than direct cell replacement.
  • This offers hope for the restoration of visual function that was previously unattainable.

WJ-MSC therapy not only improves vision but also brings hope for stopping progression towards blindness.

In clinical trials and real-world case reports, Wharton's Jelly-derived Mesenchymal Stem Cell (WJ-MSC) therapy has demonstrated improvement in vision and visual field.

These changes instill in patients the realization that previously impossible tasks are now gradually becoming feasible.

WJ-MSC therapy is not only anticipated to improve but also possesses the ability to halt progression. For example:

  • Long-term follow-up research report demonstrating preserved vision and visual field for 3 years.

The cessation or slowing of progression, previously difficult to achieve, is now a reality through WJ-MSC therapy.

Improvements in vision and retinal health have been confirmed following Stem Cell Therapy across all these conditions, and it is hypothesized that the fundamental capacity to repair damaged tissue functions irrespective of the etiology of blindness.

Safety and Absence of Rejection Risk in Treatment

WJ-MSC therapy has very high safety, and the risk of immune rejection and cancerization is almost non-existent, which has been confirmed through numerous clinical studies.

When undergoing regenerative therapy, the most critical question is, "Is it truly safe?" In this regard, Wharton's Jelly-derived Mesenchymal Stem Cell (WJ-MSC) therapy is a highly safe option, substantiated by extensive clinical research.

The contributions of others are not easily disregarded.

Umbilical cord-derived WJ-MSCs possess characteristics that do not readily induce immune rejection. Typically, when allogeneic cells are introduced into the body, the immune system mounts a response, recognizing them as foreign entities. However, WJ-MSCs exhibit minimal expression of immunogenic markers on their cell surface, thereby facilitating their acceptance by the body.

Across all MSC clinical trials conducted worldwide, no serious immune rejection cases have been reported.

The risk of malignant transformation is also very low.

Stem cells possess a high proliferative capacity, which sometimes raises concerns about malignant transformation. However, it has been unequivocally confirmed that WJ-MSCs are non-tumorigenic.

No reports of abnormal masses or growths have emerged post-administration, and furthermore, large-scale analyses have not indicated an increased risk of cancer following MSC therapy.

No Side Effects and Naturally Compatible with the Body

In clinical trials, no serious adverse events were identified ocularly or systemically.

For example, in a study involving over 80 participants in Turkey, no serious adverse events occurred during the 6-month to 1-year follow-up period following WJ-MSC transplantation.

Upon the introduction of WJ-MSC into the body, it neither induces inflammation nor toxicity. Following the exertion of its therapeutic effects, the cell is naturally absorbed and eliminated. Its mode of action can be described as: naturally integrating with the body, performing its requisite functions, and subsequently dissipating discreetly.

Key Point

  • WJ-MSC exhibit low immunogenicity and can be safely administered even from allogeneic donors.
  • There is no risk of malignant transformation (tumor transformation), and its safety has been confirmed.
  • No serious side effects have been reported, and it is naturally absorbed by the body.
  • Robust safety has been substantiated by global clinical trials.
  • Treatment that demonstrates therapeutic efficacy and is well-tolerated.
