Blindness no longer signifies a definitive end. With advancements in regenerative medicine, those who have lost their sight now possess the potential to experience light once more.

Stem cells derived from Wharton's Jelly, a tissue found within the umbilical cord of infants, offer promise for restoring some lost visual function or halting disease progression.

Aspiration for renewed well-being

Melalui terapi WJ-MSC (Sel Punca Mesenkimal dari Wharton's Jelly), perubahan positif seperti "merasakan cahaya" dan "pandangan yang lebih terang" telah dilaporkan.

For individuals who have lost their vision, even the ability to perceive minimal light is an invaluable source of hope. Through the power of stem cells via WJ-MSC therapy, patients have reported experiences described as 'light gradually entering a world that was previously dark.'

For example, some patients undergoing therapy reported improvements such as "clearer color differentiation" and "the ability to sense approaching vehicles from a distance."

Additionally, reading speed for text on smartphones improves, and morning vision becomes clearer, with effects lasting several hours.

It is akin to a closed door gradually opening, with light beginning to emerge.

Although a minor change, for this patient, it signifies a crucial first step out of complete darkness, profoundly impacting their daily life and emotional well-being.

Item

  • WJ-MSC therapy has reported experiences of "light perception" and "brighter vision."
  • Patients are better able to distinguish colors and recognize motion.
  • Enhanced smartphone text reading speed and clearer morning vision.
  • Even in total blindness, partial vision recovery remains possible.
  • Minor changes can have a significant positive impact on patients' lives and emotional well-being.

Regenerative Power in the Umbilical Cord – What are Wharton's Jelly Stem Cells

Sel Punca Mesenkimal dari Wharton's Jelly (WJ-MSC) adalah "sel harapan" dalam kedokteran regeneratif yang menggabungkan kemampuan perbaikan luar biasa dengan keamanan tinggi.

Mesenchymal Stem Cells from Wharton's Jelly (WJ-MSC) are specialized stem cells isolated from the gelatinous tissue known as Wharton's Jelly, found within the umbilical cord of newborns. Although the umbilical cord is typically discarded after birth, it contains stem cells with a potent capacity to repair and regenerate various body tissues.

WJ-MSCs are classified as mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), known for their versatility in repairing various tissues, including neural, vascular, bone, and cartilage. Furthermore, they can be expanded in large quantities using culture technology, thereby ensuring a sufficient supply for therapeutic applications.

A key consideration is that this cell possesses "immunological privilege," meaning it does not readily elicit rejection reactions even when transplanted from another person. This is because the surface of WJ-MSCs has very few "immune markers" (such as MHC class II molecules), which supports its high safety profile in clinical applications.

Furthermore, it has been demonstrated that the risk of tumor (cancer) transformation from WJ-MSCs is very low, with no reported cases of patients experiencing abnormal growths or lumps post-therapy. Ethically, there are also no concerns, as the cells are derived from umbilical cords that would otherwise be discarded, and this cell source is considered safe for use.

Consequently, WJ-MSC, which integrates safety, efficacy, and ethical considerations, is currently attracting significant attention in regenerative medicine clinics worldwide. It is considered a pivotal "key" for restoring vision in challenging conditions such as blindness.

Item

  • WJ-MSC is a stem cell derived from infant umbilical cord with high regenerative capabilities.
  • Possesses 'immunological privilege,' making it less prone to rejection even from an allogeneic donor.
  • The risk of tumor transformation is very low, and its safety is excellent.
  • The source of these cells is ethically sound, as they are derived from umbilical cords that would otherwise be discarded.
  • Garnering international attention in regenerative medicine as a "key" to restoring vision.

Mechanism of Stem Cell Therapy for Ocular Conditions

WJ-MSC bekerja sebagai "pendukung" yang melindungi retina yang rusak, mengaktifkannya kembali, dan membangkitkan kekuatan untuk merasakan cahaya lagi.

Blindness primarily results from damage or degeneration of retinal nerve cells (photoreceptor cells or optic nerve fibers) and retinal pigment epithelial cells, leading to impaired function. In this context, Wharton's Jelly-derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells (WJ-MSCs) play a crucial role. WJ-MSCs support injured ocular cells and promote the healing process towards functional recovery.

The strengths demonstrated by WJ-MSC can be divided into two major categories.

Cellular Repair Support (Paracrine Effect)

WJ-MSCs secrete abundant cytokines and growth factors to stimulate surrounding cells. These growth factors act directly on the retina to:

  • Alleviate inflammation
  • Preventing cell death (apoptosis)
  • Reducing oxidative stress (cellular corrosion)
  • Enhance blood flow and nutrient supply

Fostering the creation of an optimal environment for regeneration.

Additionally, WJ-MSCs possess a unique function, transferring their mitochondria (the cell's powerhouses) to damaged cells to provide supplemental energy. This mechanism enables compromised photoreceptor cells to regain activity and enhances the function of surviving cells.

Transformation into Eye Cells (Differentiation Capacity)

Furthermore, certain WJ-MSCs have been reported to exhibit properties akin to photoreceptor cells or retinal pigment epithelial cells in vivo. In animal models, injected MSCs demonstrated a light-sensing response after engraftment into the retina, holding significant promise for human application.

At present, WJ-MSCs are understood to facilitate the strengthening of surrounding cells and stimulate intrinsic repair, rather than directly replacing lost cells. Nevertheless, even this approach alone has brought hope for the restoration of visual function previously unattainable through conventional methods, and represents a powerful force in advancing the future of regenerative medicine.

Item

  • WJ-MSC secretes cytokines and growth factors to suppress retinal inflammation and promote repair.
  • Transferring mitochondria into damaged cells to provide additional energy.
  • Some WJ-MSCs may possess properties similar to photoreceptor cells or retinal cells.
  • The primary effect is "helping surrounding cells to become stronger and triggering self-repair" rather than direct cell replacement.
  • This offers hope for the restoration of previously unattainable visual function.

In clinical trials and real-world case reports, Wharton's Jelly Mesenchymal Stem Cell (WJ-MSC) therapy demonstrated improvements in vision and visual field.

The main changes reported are:

  • Retinal function was also confirmed to have recovered through electroretinogram (ERG) testing.

Effect of halting or decelerating development.

WJ-MSC therapy is not only anticipated to improve but also possesses the ability to halt progression. For example:

  • A case of genetic retinal disease exhibiting confirmed non-progression over a one-year period.
  • An example where the field of view and scotopic vision slightly improved
  • A long-term follow-up research report demonstrating preserved vision and visual field for 3 years.

The arrest or deceleration of progression, previously difficult to achieve, is now a reality with WJ-MSC therapy.

Improvements in vision and retinal health have been confirmed following Stem Cell Therapy across all these conditions, and it is hypothesized that the fundamental ability to "repair damaged tissue" operates irrespective of the etiology of blindness.

Safety and Absence of Rejection Risk in Medical Treatment

Terapi WJ-MSC memiliki keamanan yang sangat tinggi, dan risiko penolakan imun serta kankerisasi hampir tidak ada, yang telah dipastikan melalui banyak penelitian klinis.

A paramount concern when considering regenerative therapy is its safety. In this regard, Wharton's Jelly-derived Mesenchymal Stem Cell (WJ-MSC) therapy offers a highly secure option, substantiated by extensive clinical research.

Cells from others are not easily rejected.

WJ-MSCs, derived from the umbilical cord, are characterized by low immunogenicity. Typically, when allogeneic cells are introduced into the body, the immune system perceives them as foreign and mounts an attack. However, WJ-MSCs exhibit minimal expression of immunogenic markers on their cell surface, thereby facilitating their acceptance by the body.

In MSC clinical trials conducted globally, not a single serious case of immune rejection has been reported.

The risk of malignant transformation is also very low.

Stem cells possess a high proliferative capacity, which sometimes raises concerns about malignant transformation. However, it has been definitively confirmed that WJ-MSCs are non-tumorigenic.

There have been no reports of post-administration masses or abnormal growths, and furthermore, large-scale analyses have yielded no data indicating an increased risk of cancer following MSC therapy.

No Side Effects and Naturally Compatible with the Body

In clinical trials, no serious adverse events were identified ocularly or systemically.

For example, in a study involving over 80 participants in Turkey, not a single serious adverse event occurred during the 6-month to 1-year follow-up after WJ-MSC transplantation.

When WJ-MSCs are introduced into the body, they do not induce inflammation or toxicity. After exerting their therapeutic effects, the cells are naturally resorbed and cleared. Their operational principle is best described as 'naturally compatible with the body, performing the necessary work, and then quietly disappearing.'

Item

  • WJ-MSC exhibit low immunogenicity and can be safely utilized even from allogeneic donors.
  • There is no risk of malignant transformation, and its safety has been confirmed.
  • No serious adverse events have been reported, and it is naturally absorbed by the body.
  • Robust safety has been substantiated by global clinical trials.
  • Treatment that demonstrates therapeutic efficacy without imposing undue burden on the body.
