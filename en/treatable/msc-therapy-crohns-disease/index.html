Crohn's Disease and Current Treatment Challenges

Crohn's disease is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease characterized by persistent inflammation of the gastrointestinal tract. This condition manifests with symptoms such as abdominal pain, diarrhea, and recurrent weight loss. The disease is marked by recurring cycles of remission (when symptoms subside) and active periods (when symptoms worsen), and it is considered challenging to achieve a fundamental cure.

Current therapeutic approaches primarily focus on managing inflammation through pharmacological and surgical interventions when indicated. However, each presents its own challenges.

Challenges of Drug Therapy

Steroids, immunomodulatory drugs, and biological agents (such as anti-TNF antibodies) are commonly employed to suppress inflammation. While these medications can alleviate symptoms and induce remission in many cases, their long-term use is associated with adverse effects (such as an increased risk of infection and osteoporosis), and drug responsiveness gradually diminishes, leading to reduced efficacy.

Furthermore, while the medication alleviates symptoms, it does not cure the underlying disease, thus the potential for recurrence remains.

Challenges in Surgical Therapy

When complications such as severe intestinal strictures or fistulas (abnormal connections, often involving pus) arise, surgical intervention is performed to resect the diseased segment or close the fistula. Although surgery can offer temporary symptomatic improvement, it places a substantial burden on the body, and the potential for recurrence remains. As Crohn's disease can manifest in any part of the gastrointestinal tract, inflammation may subsequently develop in other areas post-operatively.

While the underlying cause remains unaddressed, lifelong treatment is required. Consequently, current medical approaches primarily focus on symptomatic therapy, aimed at suppressing symptoms, which inherently limits efforts towards a complete cure. Both patients and their families must contend with concerns about side effects and the fear of recurrence. Furthermore, many are burdened by the persistent question: "Will this condition continue indefinitely?"

In this area, over the past few years, stem cell therapy and other regenerative medicine have garnered significant interest as novel therapeutic options.

New Possibilities of Stem Cell Therapy and Regenerative Medicine for Crohn's Disease

Regenerative medicine is a medical field that leverages the body's natural repair and regeneration capabilities to treat diseases. Central to this are cells known as stem cells.

Stem cells are cells that serve as the origin for various cell types in the body and possess the ability to repair damaged tissues and regenerate bodily functions. Among these, a type of stem cell known as mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) exhibit immunomodulatory and tissue repair properties, and for inflammatory diseases such as Crohn's disease...

Currently, stem cell therapy offers new potential for Crohn's disease. If traditional drug therapy is characterized as 'forcibly suppressing the immune system's runaway inflammatory response with medication,' then stem cell therapy, conversely, is an approach that 'harnesses the body's naturally inherent reparative and immunomodulatory functions.'

Administering autologous or allogeneic stem cells into the body via infusion and other methods is expected to mitigate excessive inflammation and promote the regeneration of damaged tissue. In essence, this therapeutic approach modulates the dysregulated immune response in Crohn's disease and facilitates intestinal mucosal healing.

There are various types of stem cells, and mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) can be harvested from diverse tissues such as bone marrow, adipose tissue, and the umbilical cord. Of particular interest recently are Wharton's jelly-derived mesenchymal stem cells (WJ-MSCs), which are stem cells isolated from the gelatinous tissue known as Wharton's jelly within the umbilical cord.

These WJ-MSCs offer the advantage that, being derived from umbilical cords discarded after birth, their extraction is painless and ethically unproblematic. The obtained cells are also young, exhibiting enhanced proliferative capacity, and are immunologically less prone to rejection due to their immunoprivileged status and low immunogenicity.

Given these characteristics, WJ-MSCs are anticipated as a novel cell source in regenerative medicine and are now being implemented in stem cell therapy for Crohn's disease.

Mechanism of Action of Stem Cell Therapy for Crohn's Disease

How exactly do stem cells function upon entering the body? To facilitate a clearer understanding, let us explain this by drawing an analogy to a car's braking system and a repair workshop.

In Crohn's disease, the immune system, which is intended to protect the body, instead becomes misdirected, debilitating and attacking the body's own intestines, leading to inflammation. This condition is analogous to a car operating with faulty brakes.

Typically, the immune system possesses an 'accelerator role' that promotes inflammation and a 'brake role' that suppresses it. However, in Crohn's disease, the 'brake' function is impaired, allowing the 'accelerator' to be fully engaged.

In this context, stem cells function as an intrinsic "braking mechanism" within the body, effectively halting debilitating immune responses. Specifically, mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) release "calming" signals into their microenvironment, gently modulating runaway immune reactions. This process is analogous to installing new brakes on a vehicle experiencing uncontrolled acceleration to restore control and reduce speed.

Furthermore, concerning damaged intestinal mucosa resulting from leakage, stem cells also serve as "repairing agents." MSCs accumulate at the damaged tissue and secrete substances that aid in its repair.

This collection of substances is termed the secretome, which encompasses various reparative factors, including inflammation-modulating cytokines and growth factors. This creates an environment conducive to the regeneration of intestinal tissue damaged by inflammation.

Furthermore, MSCs themselves possess the ability to differentiate into intestinal cells as needed, and may directly support healing at the site of damage.

Secara ilmiah, sedikit suplemen: MSC bertindak seperti pusat komando yang mengatur keseimbangan sel imun. Sebagai contoh, dilaporkan bahwa ia meningkatkan makrofag anti-peradangan (makrofag M2) dan menekan kerja berlebihan sel seperti neutrofil dan sel dendriti yang memicu peradangan. Lebih jauh, ia juga bekerja pada sel T dan sel B, yang merupakan komandan imun, mengurangi pelepasan zat peradangan (sitokin) yang mereka hasilkan untuk mengerem pelarian imun. Di sisi lain, untuk mempromosikan perbaikan jaringan yang rusak, ia juga menyediakan zat yang menghasilkan pembuluh darah baru dan faktor pertumbuhan yang membantu regenerasi jaringan.

Through this complex mechanism, stem cell therapy is believed to demonstrate the "two birds with one stone" effect of "calming inflammation while healing tissue." In essence, stem cell therapy is a treatment method that simultaneously plays an immunomodulatory role and a role in promoting tissue regeneration. By modulating the dysregulated immune system in Crohn's disease and facilitating the repair of damaged intestinal tissue, it is expected to achieve long-term remission (a state where symptoms are quiescent over an extended period) and mucosal healing, which has previously been challenging to achieve.

Evidence of Effectiveness of Stem Cell Therapy for Crohn's Disease

Results of Meta-Analysis (Combined Analysis of Multiple Experiments):

According to an analysis combining several clinical trials published in 2024, when stem cell therapy was administered to patients with difficult-to-treat Crohn's disease for whom conventional treatments were insufficiently effective, the remission rate (defined as symptom resolution) was approximately twofold higher compared to conventional treatment alone. This analysis synthesized data from 12 randomized trials (totaling 632 participants), yielding results with high scientific confidence.

It is also important to note that, regarding safety as detailed subsequently, the incidence of serious adverse events was comparable to that in the control group, indicating that while efficacy was enhanced, safety remained uncompromised.

Specific Achievements of Clinical Trials

Numerous clinical trials have also been reported investigating stem cell therapy in patients with Crohn's disease. For example, in a randomized controlled trial conducted in China, which involved the infusion of umbilical cord-derived mesenchymal stem cells (WJ-MSCs), significant improvements in symptom scores and a reduction in the required steroid dosage were observed in the stem cell-treated group. It was concluded that "stem cell therapy can safely improve the disease state."

Furthermore, other research has reported cases where intestinal endoscopic findings improved following stem cell therapy, with mucosal healing, and cases where difficult-to-treat fistulas closed. Sample International Agreement

The efficacy of stem cell therapy is progressively being integrated into medical practice. A notable example is its approval in Europe. In 2018, a stem cell product marketed as Alofisel® (darvadstrocel) was approved in Europe for the treatment of complex perianal fistulas associated with Crohn's disease. This product involves a treatment where allogeneic adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) are cultured and injected into the affected area. It is indicated for patients with refractory fistulas where existing treatments (such as anti-TNF agents) have been ineffective.

The European Medicines Agency (EMA) acknowledges the indication that "Alofisel can be used for complex anal fistulas that are refractory to conventional therapy," and is noted as the world's first stem cell therapy drug. This approval serves as evidence that the efficacy of stem cell therapy is gaining scientific and clinical recognition.

This evidence supports the promise that stem cell therapy offers to patients with Crohn's disease. It is important to note, however, that much of this is currently in clinical trial phases, limited to specific cases, and not yet a panacea. Nevertheless, a substantial body of evidence has accumulated, fostering considerable hope that it "may become a means to cure what was previously incurable."

Stem cell therapy is currently under continuous investigation. In the future, the scope of its effectiveness, duration, and other factors will be further elucidated.

Safety and Side Effects of Stem Cell Therapy for Crohn's Disease

When considering new treatments, patients' primary concern is safety and side effects. The currently reported safety profile for stem cell therapy is excellent.

In national and international clinical studies, as described above, serious adverse events have been rarely reported. For instance, in the aforementioned 2024 meta-analysis, no significant difference in the incidence rate of serious adverse events (such as infection or cancer) was observed between patients receiving stem cell therapy and those undergoing conventional treatment only, leading to the conclusion that safety concerns were not elevated.

Stem cell therapy is essentially a treatment that utilizes cells from the patient's own body or human-derived cells. Consequently, it is believed that the risk of systemic adverse effects from chemical agents, such as gastrointestinal disturbances or liver damage, commonly associated with conventional drug therapies, is lower.

Upon administration of cells into the body via infusion or injection, transient febrile reactions, headaches, or allergic responses are rare. When observed, these are predominantly mild and temporary. Furthermore, even when utilizing allogeneic stem cells, Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs) inherently exhibit low immunogenicity, thereby minimizing the risk of rejection reactions and enabling the safe use of allogeneic cells.

Specifically, cells such as WJ-MSCs, characterized by low immunogenicity, have been confirmed to be well-tolerated by patients, even when derived from an allogeneic donor. Furthermore, in clinical trials and post-marketing data for the Alofisel® MSC preparation approved in Europe, no major safety concerns have been reported. Regarding long-term safety, while observation and research are ongoing, reports indicate that patients have experienced no serious complications over a multi-year timeframe.

In contrast, potent conventional drugs such as steroids carry various risks of adverse effects from long-term use, including infections and lifestyle-related diseases. Therefore, if stem cell therapy can reduce or discontinue these medications, the overall risk of adverse effects could potentially be lowered.

Overall, stem cell therapy presents an exceptional balance of both effectiveness and safety. Naturally, no treatment is "completely safe," but to date, no serious adverse effects unique to stem cell therapy have been reported. We have therefore reached a stage where we can confidently proceed with clinical implementation.

Patients participating in clinical trials also reported experiencing no significant discomfort from the treatment and that they only received an infusion with minimal physical burden. Compared to conventional surgery and other interventions, this approach can be characterized as a well-tolerated treatment.
