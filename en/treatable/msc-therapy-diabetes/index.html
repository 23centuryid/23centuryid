Stem Cell Therapy for Diabetes

  • Home

  • Stem Cell Therapy for Diabetes

Diabetes is a medical condition characterized by chronically elevated blood glucose levels, classified as Type 1 and Type 2.

In Type 1 diabetes, an autoimmune disorder destroys insulin-producing cells, consequently necessitating lifelong insulin therapy. In Type 2, insulin resistance increases and production decreases, which may require insulin therapy as the disease progresses.

In both cases, treatment primarily focuses on glycemic control, and it is challenging to fully prevent long-term complications. Addressing this challenge, in recent years, regenerative therapies aimed at "fundamental repair" have garnered attention.

Furthermore, stem cell therapy (MSC therapy), which aims for the simultaneous restoration of insulin secretion and modulation of the immune environment, is expected to be a promising new treatment option.

Stem Cell Therapy as Regenerative Therapy

Terapi sel punca adalah metode pengobatan baru yang bertujuan untuk perbaikan mendasar diabetes dengan memulihkan fungsi sel yang memproduksi insulin dan mengeluarkan kekuatan penyembuhan tubuh.

Conventional diabetes treatment has historically focused on a "management" approach to control blood glucose levels. However, with the growing need for a "fundamental therapy" that restores pancreatic function, regenerative therapy is garnering increasing attention.

Regenerative medicine is a medical field dedicated to repairing and regenerating damaged organ or tissue function by harnessing the body's intrinsic capabilities. In the context of diabetes, various regenerative techniques are under investigation with the goal of restoring the function of insulin-producing pancreatic beta-cells.

Among all available options, stem cell therapy has shown the most promising results. Stem cells possess the ability to differentiate into other cell types or secrete essential factors, and are considered the cellular basis for tissue regeneration.

Of particular focus are cells known as Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs). MSCs can be extracted from tissues such as bone marrow, adipose tissue, and umbilical cord, and are characterized by high proliferative capacity as well as the ability to differentiate into various cell types (pluripotent).

In the field of diabetes, MSCs are expected to possess the following two characteristics:

  • Potential for differentiation into beta-cells and addressing the deficiency in insulin secretion.
  • To release cytokines and growth factors to prepare the surrounding tissue environment and to stimulate self-healing capabilities.

Notably, Wharton's Jelly-derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs) offer significant advantages, particularly concerning their superior proliferative capacity and minimal immunogenicity. As they are extracted from the umbilical cords of newborns, these cells are inherently nascent and are reported to rarely elicit allogeneic rejection responses.

Furthermore, autologous cell therapy may necessitate surgical extraction, whereas allogeneic Wharton's jelly MSCs can be safely utilized, thereby significantly reducing the physical burden on the body.

As such, MSCs are not merely envisioned as a "replacement" therapy, but rather as one that activates the body's intrinsic regenerative power, and are increasingly drawing attention as a novel option for diabetes.

  • The goal in regenerative therapy for diabetes is the restoration of function of insulin-producing pancreatic islet β-cells.
  • Mesengimal Stem Cells (MSCs) are expected to have regenerative effects stemming from both their pluripotentiality and secretory capabilities.
  • MSC serves two functions: to replenish β-cells and to modulate the tissue environment.
  • Wharton's jelly mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), derived from young umbilical cords, exhibit low immunogenicity and are safe for allogeneic use.
  • The fact that it can be administered non-surgically and through less invasive means also establishes it as a therapeutic method that imposes minimal burden on the patient.

MSC-mediated Multifaceted Mechanism of Action

MSC adalah pendekatan terapi yang dapat bertindak secara bersamaan dari berbagai sudut pandang terhadap berbagai tantangan dalam diabetes termasuk kelainan imun, kerusakan fungsi pankreas, dan peradangan kronis.

MSC therapy's effectiveness extends beyond mere cell replacement. From the local pancreatic level to the systemic metabolic environment, "adjustment, restoration, and activation" can be achieved across a broad spectrum, truly playing a role akin to a "multi-function switch" in diabetes treatment.

Here, we will explain three representative actions taken by MSC.

Immune Regulation (Autoimmune Suppression)

In Type 1 Diabetes, pancreatic β-cells are destroyed by autoimmune mechanisms. MSCs secrete anti-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-10 and TGF-β, which have the effect of tempering excessive immune activation.

Specifically, this has been confirmed to enhance regulatory T cell (Treg) populations and suppress the production of inflammatory T-cell subsets (Th1/Th17 axis) and IFN-γ. It is anticipated to ameliorate the highly aggressive immune milieu surrounding the pancreatic islets.

This function serves as a regulatory mechanism that curbs an immune system on the verge of becoming overactive.

Promotion of Tissue Repair and Regeneration

MSCs secrete growth factors such as VEGF and HGF, which enhance damaged tissue repair and angiogenesis. In the pancreas, in addition to supporting the survival and functional maintenance of residual β-cells, reports also indicate the potential to promote the regeneration of new insulin-secreting β-cells.

In experiments, instances have also been reported where MSCs differentiated into β-like cells capable of insulin secretion. In other words, this can be likened to dispatching a repair team to a damaged cellular factory to support its operational restoration.

Suppression of Inflammation and Optimization of the Metabolic Environment

In type 2 diabetes, chronic inflammation underlies insulin resistance. Anti-inflammatory cytokines secreted by MSCs (such as IL-10) suppress the excessive production of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and IL-1β.

Furthermore, by altering the composition of immune cells from pro-inflammatory to inhibitory types, the systemic metabolic environment transforms into a "state of heightened insulin sensitivity." This mechanism is akin to "repairing communication pathways" in a condition where whole-body metabolic signals are inadequately transmitted.

As previously explained, MSC therapy not only targets the pancreas but also exerts systemic effects, influencing immunity, inflammation, and metabolism. This capacity to simultaneously modulate multiple biological circuits represents a significant advantage unique to stem cell therapy compared to other treatments.

Item

  • MSCs exhibit activity in suppressing the autoimmune reactions that are problematic in type 1 diabetes.
  • Promoting the protection and regeneration of damaged pancreatic beta-cells, contributing to the restoration of insulin secretion function.
  • By suppressing chronic inflammation and reducing insulin resistance, it also addresses the metabolic environment of type 2 diabetes.
  • A key strength of MSCs is their capacity to simultaneously address the immune system, pancreas, and metabolism at different levels.

Effects of Stem Cell Therapy According to Clinical Research

Effect on Type 1 Diabetes

MSC dapat menyebabkan peningkatan fungsi sekresi insulin dan pengurangan suntikan insulin, dengan hasil yang positif terutama dilaporkan pada pasien dalam fase awal penyakit.

In clinical trials of MSC therapy for type 1 diabetes, various studies have confirmed improved glycemic control and reduced insulin requirements. Notably, numerous instances have been observed where an increase in C-peptide (a marker indicating endogenous insulin production) and an improvement in HbA1c were noted, and it has also demonstrated the potential for insulin injections to become unnecessary.

In fact, one study reported that approximately 20% of patients who received MSC transplantation were able to discontinue insulin injections, and many other patients also experienced a significant reduction in their required dosage.

Reports also indicate that efficacy varies depending on the type of stem cell. It has been reported that umbilical cord-derived MSCs (Wharton's jelly MSCs) may lead to a greater reduction in HbA1c and an increase in C-peptide compared to those derived from bone marrow, and are superior in preserving pancreatic β-cell function.

Long-term effectiveness has also been reported, with approximately 80% of patients undergoing MSC therapy maintaining stable HbA1c levels for over 3 years. Typically, glycemic control tends to deteriorate over time, making this a significant observation. A slower decline in C-peptide was also observed, suggesting a potential for preserving pancreatic function.

Furthermore, an analysis evaluating various clinical trials also concluded that MSC is likely beneficial in reducing insulin requirements and stabilizing glycemic control.

Item

  • MSC injections have been reported to increase C-peptide and increase HbA1c.
  • Efficacy is suggested, with approximately 20% of patients no longer requiring insulin injections.
  • MSCs derived from the umbilical cord may have higher efficacy than those derived from bone marrow.
  • There are also instances of maintaining stable HbA1c levels for over 3 years, and the long-term effects have garnered considerable attention.
  • Various combined clinical studies support a reduction in insulin requirements and improved glycemic control.

Effect on Type 2 Diabetes

Pada diabetes tipe 2 juga, terapi MSC ditunjukkan untuk memiliki potensi dalam membantu stabilisasi gula darah, mengurangi resistensi insulin dan peradangan, serta menekan risiko komplikasi.

In studies focused on Type 2 Diabetes, there is a growing number of reports detailing improved blood glucose management through MSC injections. Particularly, in patients with severe disease requiring insulin, the restoration of insulin secretion and stabilization of blood glucose have been confirmed.

In one clinical trial, following the injection of placenta-derived MSCs, insulin requirements were halved, and an increase in C-peptide levels was also confirmed. In a separate trial, utilizing umbilical cord-derived MSCs, 41% of patients were able to discontinue insulin injections, and 94% reported a reduction in insulin use.

Furthermore, improvements were also observed in indicators related to immunity and inflammation. Following MSC injection, inflammatory T-cell activity was suppressed, and the levels of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and IL-1β also decreased. These changes are believed to contribute to improved insulin resistance.

Furthermore, a suppressive effect on the risk of developing complications has also been reported. In studies involving umbilical cord-derived MSCs, 30% of patients no longer required insulin treatment for over one year post-therapy, and complications such as nephropathy and neuropathy were also reported to be less frequent.

Given the beneficial effects not only on glycemic control but also on the overall metabolic profile of the body and quality of life, MSC therapy is garnering attention as a novel therapeutic option for diabetes.

Item

  • In severe type 2 diabetes, there have been instances of significant reductions in insulin use.
  • Elevations in C-peptide and HbA1c were reported, and a positive effect on glycemic control was demonstrated.
  • Suppression of inflammation and increased insulin resistance have been confirmed.
  • The mitigation of complication risks and the long-term stabilization of blood glucose are also anticipated.
  • By addressing the pancreatic, immune, and metabolic aspects, it is gaining attention as a multifunctional therapy.

Safety and Risks of Side Effects of Stem Cell Therapy

Terapi MSC adalah metode pengobatan dengan keamanan tinggi yang telah dikonfirmasi untuk diabetes. Laporan tentang efek samping yang parah sangat jarang, dan karena beban pada tubuh ringan, semakin mendapat perhatian.

Upon the introduction of novel therapeutic methods, safety is the paramount consideration. Across numerous conducted clinical trials involving mesenchymal stem cell (MSC) treatments, there have been virtually no reports of severe adverse events or complications. Both domestic and international research has established that MSC injections in diabetic patients demonstrate a high safety profile.

Notably, umbilical cord-derived MSCs (Wharton's jelly MSCs) utilized in our facility have not reported any adverse events in actual clinical applications, thereby validating their high safety profile. Furthermore, a recent meta-analysis also evaluated MSC therapy for type 1 and type 2 diabetes as a therapeutic approach demonstrating both efficacy and safety.

Underlying this safety is the low immunogenicity of MSCs. Typically, when allogeneic cells are introduced into the body, an immune rejection response occurs. However, MSCs possess the characteristic of low Human Leukocyte Antigen (HLA) expression on their cell surface, making them difficult for the immune system to recognize. Consequently, even with allogeneic cells, injections can be safely administered without the need for specific immunosuppressive agents.

Furthermore, MSC therapy is highly regarded for its minimal physiological burden. This treatment is primarily administered via intravenous infusion, though local injections are also possible, all without the invasiveness typically associated with general surgical procedures. Consequently, in many instances, the procedure can be completed in an outpatient setting, significantly reducing the patient's burden by eliminating the need for hospitalization.

From this perspective, stem cell therapy is an advanced therapy for diabetes that, due to its rare side effects and low invasiveness, warrants substantial consideration.

Item

  • In MSC therapy, reports of serious side effects or adverse events are very rare.
  • Umbilical cord-derived MSCs have a particularly strong track record regarding safety and stability.
  • MSC exhibit low immunogenicity and are resistant to rejection, thus enabling their administration without immunosuppressive agents.
  • Infusion administration is a minimally invasive treatment gaining attention as an outpatient therapy.
  • Future broader introduction as a treatment option combining safety and practicality is anticipated.

The Future of Diabetes Treatment

Stem cell therapy is a regenerative therapy that aims for the fundamental amelioration of diabetes by simultaneously promoting pancreatic islet cell regeneration and immune normalization.

Domestic and international research has consistently reported positive outcomes regarding both safety and efficacy, and progress towards future practical implementation is steadily advancing. Therapies that stabilize blood glucose levels independently of insulin, and medical interventions designed to progressively prevent the development of complications, are increasingly within reach through stem cell technology.

Given the chronic nature of the disease, novel options targeting fundamental improvements will offer hope.
