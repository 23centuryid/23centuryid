Spinal cord injury is a serious condition where the spinal cord is damaged as a result of a sudden traumatic event, leading to severe paralysis and impaired sensation.

In current medical treatment, the focus is on surgical bone fixation, pharmacological suppression of inflammation, and rehabilitation to prevent further progression. However, a therapeutic method that truly "cures" lost nerve function has not yet been established.

Consequently, patients with spinal cord injuries face long-term consequences such as paralysis. In recent years, however, Stem Cell Therapy, as a "regenerative medicine," has garnered significant attention, opening new avenues for pursuing recovery without succumbing to spinal cord injuries.

Below, we explain current treatment challenges, the novel prospects of Stem Cell Therapy, its mechanism of action, evidence of effectiveness, safety, and related topics, presented in a manner easily understandable by the general public.

Spinal Cord Injury and Current Treatment Challenges

The spinal cord can be described as the "main neural highway" that carries commands from the brain to the body.

When this spinal cord is damaged, signals to the neural circuits below it are severed. This is akin to a major highway being cut off by a traffic accident, preventing the transmission of signals from the brain.

Consequently, in regions caudal to the site of damage, motor and sensory functions of the limbs are compromised, leading to paralysis. Additionally, autonomic nervous system functions, including micturition, defecation, and perspiration, are also impaired, significantly impacting quality of life.

In current standard medical practice, initially, in the acute post-injury phase, surgical intervention is performed to decompress the spinal cord from fractured bone fragments and protruding intervertebral discs, and the spine is stabilized. Concurrently, treatments such as steroid administration are initiated to mitigate the progression of secondary injury (i.e., neurological damage resulting from inflammation and edema).

Subsequently, rehabilitation became the focus, training the body to maximize movement utilizing residual neurological function.

However, once severed, spinal cord nerve fibers exhibit limited natural regeneration, making it challenging to fully restore lost function with current treatments alone.

The inadequacy of effective treatment for spinal cord injury symptoms poses a significant challenge in Japan, affecting over 150,000 patients nationwide, with approximately 5,000 new cases reported annually.

Nevertheless, novel approaches offering hope for patients and their families are emerging.

It was previously stated that "recovery cannot be expected in the chronic phase (the state after a period has elapsed since the injury)," but recent research findings are beginning to challenge this conventional wisdom. With the advent of Stem Cell Therapy, the potential for functional improvement has been observed, even in individuals with long-standing spinal cord injuries.

Spinal Cord Injury and Stem Cell Therapy: New Possibilities for Regenerative Medicine

Stem Cell Therapy is a medical treatment method that utilizes stem cells, which possess the capacity to differentiate into various cell types within the body or facilitate tissue repair, thereby promoting the regeneration of damaged tissues.

As a regenerative therapy for spinal cord injuries, treatments involving the transplantation of stem cells directly to the site of spinal cord damage or their systemic administration via infusion are currently being researched and implemented worldwide.

There are several types of stem cells. For example, stem cells derived from the patient's own bone marrow or fat, or stem cells obtained from the umbilical cords of other donors.

Among these, umbilical cord-derived Wharton's Jelly mesenchymal stem cells (WJ-MSCs) possess high proliferative and differentiation capacities, along with low immunogenicity, and are considered promising for the treatment of spinal cord injury. (While detailed characteristics of WJ-MSCs are beyond the scope of this article, their efficacy has been indicated.)

This stem cell therapy offers the potential to repair and regenerate the damaged spinal cord itself, and is anticipated to yield improvements in paralytic function unachievable through conventional treatments.

As an emerging prospect in regenerative medicine, clinical research and certain treatments are already underway internationally. For instance, a clinical trial conducted at the Mayo Clinic in the United States demonstrated that treatment using autologous adipose-derived stem cells resulted in an improvement in paralysis severity in 7 out of 10 patients, with safety also being confirmed.

Also in Japan, Keio University conducted the world's first trial involving the transplantation of iPS cell-derived neural precursor cells, with reports indicating improved motor function in 2 of 4 spinal cord injury patients.

As such, stem cell-based regenerative medicine is garnering global interest as a promising new avenue for spinal cord injuries.

Evidence of Effectiveness of Stem Cell Therapy for Spinal Cord Injury

Reports of genuine functional improvement in spinal cord injuries following Stem Cell Therapy are increasingly emerging worldwide, although these cases remain in the research stage. We will introduce some of this key scientific evidence.

Enhancement of sensory function (clinical trial - chronic phase):

In a clinical trial for chronic spinal cord injury conducted in Spain, treatment was administered by a single injection of umbilical cord-derived mesenchymal stem cells (WJ-MSCs) into the patient's lumbar region.

Consequently, pain perception (pinprick sensation) was confirmed to have significantly improved in the cutaneous dermatomes below the level of injury. This is a promising finding, indicating that a partial return of sensation is possible even in patients with long-standing spinal cord injuries.

Furthermore, improvements in autonomic nervous system function, such as bladder function (increased storage volume and reduced hyperactivity), were also observed in some patients.

Improvement in motor function (clinical trial - subacute to chronic phase)

In a clinical trial of adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (AD-MSCs) conducted at the Mayo Clinic in the United States (CELLBERANDA trial), stem cells were transplanted into 10 patients with traumatic spinal cord injury, and their progress was monitored for 2 years.

The results showed that in 7 out of 10 patients, neurological function grade improved by at least one level (an increase in AIS grade). Notably, one patient was identified as a "super-responder," demonstrating visible recovery of both upper and lower limb function following treatment.

Historically, it has been understood that severe paralysis resulting from spinal cord injury would not improve. However, this research demonstrates that Stem Cell Therapy can lead to an improvement in paralysis.

Comprehensive functional improvement (clinical research - Japan):

Cutting-edge clinical research in regenerative medicine is also being conducted in Japan.

In Keio University research, iPS-derived neural precursor cells were transplanted into spinal cord injury patients, and it was reported that two of four cases demonstrated improved motor function, such as walking and hand movements.

This is the world's first endeavor and is recognized as evidence originating from Japan.

Encouraging results in animal studies:

In addition to human clinical cases, extensive research is conducted on animal models.

When bone marrow- or umbilical cord-derived stem cells are administered to rat or mouse models of spinal cord injury, reports have accumulated indicating improved hindlimb motor function, as well as observed axon regeneration and reduced scarring within spinal cord tissue.

These preclinical research findings have paved the way for clinical application in humans.

Safety and Adverse Effects of Stem Cell Therapy for Spinal Cord Injury

Safety is a paramount concern when considering novel therapies. While Stem Cell Therapy is a relatively nascent field, current research demonstrates a relatively high safety profile.

Here are the key points.

Virtually no serious side effects were reported.

To date, among patients with spinal cord injury receiving Stem Cell Therapy, no severe or life-threatening adverse effects or complications have been reported.

For instance, even in the aforementioned Mayo Clinic clinical trials, no serious adverse events were observed. Similarly, in trials involving the intrathecal administration of umbilical cord-derived stem cells, no severe adverse effects were reported.

This is because stem cells are intrinsically cells native to the body. When properly prepared and managed, these cells can integrate with the human body with relative ease.

The majority of reported side effects are transient.

The majority of adverse events reported following Stem Cell Therapy are mild and transient. Specifically, headache, fever, transient blood pressure fluctuations, and injection site pain may be noted.

It typically resolves within a few days, and it is believed that symptomatic therapy with analgesics or antipyretics is sufficient.

Meticulous aseptic technique was employed for infection prevention; however, a system was established to manage unforeseen infection risks, including antibiotic administration and other interventions.

Consideration is also given to the risks of immune rejection and malignancy.

When utilizing allogeneic stem cells (e.g., umbilical cord-derived WJ-MSCs), concerns about potential rejection may arise. However, mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) are characterized by their low immunogenicity, and it is well-established that even cells from mismatched individuals are relatively well-tolerated.

Nonetheless, repeated administration may lead to antibody formation, necessitating careful consideration of treatment schedules. Furthermore, given the high proliferative rate of stem cells, the theoretical risk of malignancy (tumor transformation) is also non-zero.

However, for mesenchymal stem cells currently used clinically, no such reports of malignancy exist, and appropriate culture and management methods ensuring safety are adopted.

Overall, the safety of Stem Cell Therapy is considered good within the scope of current research.

Given that this is a novel treatment, some aspects are not yet fully elucidated. However, it is precisely for this reason that our team of experts is meticulously gathering data and endeavoring to deliver treatment with the least possible burden on patients.

The primary priority is to ensure patients receive treatment calmly, and the clinic is structured such that safety management and efficacy verification proceed in tandem.
