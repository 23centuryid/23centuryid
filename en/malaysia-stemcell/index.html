What Are Stem Cells?

Caution regarding animal-derived stem cells.

In the field of stem cell therapy, some facilities provide treatments using animal-derived stem cells. However, this method lacks adequate scientific support, and careful consideration is required regarding its safety and effectiveness.

Foremost among the risks associated with animal-derived stem cells is infection. Animals can harbor pathogens unknown to humans, which could potentially transmit infections via the stem cells. Furthermore, upon the introduction of animal cells into the body, an immune rejection response is triggered. This not only compromises therapeutic efficacy but also carries the risk of adverse health effects.

Furthermore, clinical data demonstrating the effectiveness of animal-derived stem cell treatments is very limited. Stem cell therapy originally aimed for cell engraftment and functional recovery; however, there is no guarantee that animal-derived stem cells will integrate well with human tissues and function effectively. Consequently, due to the unclear long-term effects, many medical experts adopt a cautious stance.

To ensure safe and effective treatment, it is crucial to select a clinic that utilizes human-derived stem cells. Reputable institutions clearly identify the source of their stem cells and process these cells in culture facilities that adhere to international medical standards. When evaluating stem cell therapy, it is imperative to verify the source of the cells used, the culture methods employed, and the existence of all necessary regulatory approvals, thereby ensuring treatment is administered with demonstrated safety.

Our inability to cure diseases or injuries stems from the progressively increasing difficulty in annually replacing damaged or senescent cells with new ones.

Ordinarily, damaged or senescent cells are promptly degraded and eliminated from the body. However, when the rate of cellular replacement slows, to mitigate cellular depletion, these cells must continue to be utilized within the body, even if damaged or senescent.

Cells continuously produce the substances necessary to sustain life, form tissues, and maintain organ function daily. However, as cells age, their functionality diminishes. This reduction in cell numbers and the aging process contribute to various health issues, collectively known as aging phenomena, manifesting within our bodies.
