Overview and Background

ACTD (ASEAN Common Technical Dossier) adalah format dokumen teknis bersama yang digunakan untuk pengajuan izin edar obat di negara-negara ASEAN.

In other words, it is a standardized format utilized for the registration applications of pharmaceutical products intended for human use within ASEAN member countries.

In other regions such as America, Europe, and Japan, a similar format is referred to as the CTD (Common Technical Document) by ICH; the ACTD is adapted to meet the regulatory requirements of ASEAN.

An initiative for drug regulation harmonization is underway within ASEAN, and as part of this effort, the ACTD began to be introduced in member states starting around January 2009.

In line with this, related guidelines such as long-term stability testing requirements, process validation, and bioequivalence (BA/BE) testing guidelines were also harmonized. Consequently, ASEAN member states (e.g., Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam) began mandating the submission of documents in ACTD format for drug registration applications.

The primary objective is to streamline the assessment process and reduce the administrative burden on applicants by unifying requirements that previously varied between countries. This is expected to enhance examination efficiency and reduce duplication of effort.

Furthermore, a long-term objective is to facilitate electronic submissions (eCTD) to streamline future administrative processes.

Key Requirements and Structure

ACTD adalah panduan yang menentukan struktur dokumen pengajuan (dossier) dan umumnya terdiri dari 4 bagian utama (Part I–IV).
Isi tiap bagian dirangkum sebagai berikut.

Part I: Administrative Information and Products

This section comprises the administrative documents central to the submission.

Including the application form, applicant information, product summary (name, dosage form, composition), leaflet/instructions for use, list of manufacturers, and legal documents such as declaration letters and powers of attorney, as required by local authorities.

The requested documents must comply with each country's pharmaceutical regulations. Therefore, the ACTD only provides a sample format; specific requirements may vary between countries.

Part II: Mutu (Chemical and Pharmaceutical)

Loading quality data for active pharmaceutical ingredients and drug products.

For example: active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) name, structure, manufacturing process, impurity profile, drug product formulation, production methods, test specifications, and stability test results — all Chemistry, Manufacturing and Controls (CMC) data.

Part II is mandatory for both innovator and generic products, and its structure is outlined in detail (summary, list of documents, detailed data, and references).

Part III: Non-clinical (Safety)

Contains preclinical study results, such as animal studies and in vitro tests, that support the product's safety profile.

Encompasses basic pharmacological data (pharmacodynamic studies), ADME studies, and toxicology studies (single-dose toxicity, repeated-dose toxicity, genotoxicity, reproductive toxicity, and carcinogenicity studies, if required).

This section corresponds to Module 4 of the ICH-CTD and constitutes a critical section presenting scientific evidence to support the product's safety and effectiveness.

Part IV: Clinical (Efficacy)

Loading data from human clinical trials.

Including clinical pharmacology studies (PK/PD studies), results of Phase I–III clinical trials along with integrated reports, literature evidence, and post-marketing data, if available.

This section corresponds to Module 5 of the ICH-CTD and summarizes clinical data demonstrating the efficacy and safety of the submitted drug, including clinical trial case listings and detailed statistical analysis results.

The above outlines the fundamental structure of the ACTD. Each section comprises additional specifications that applicants are required to address during the preparation of their documentation.

Perlu digarisbawahi bahwa ACTD berfungsi sebagai kerangka untuk menstandarkan struktur dokumen pengajuan, sedangkan pedoman teknis rinci mengenai isi tiap bagian diatur oleh guideline ICH atau regulasi nasional masing-masing negara. Misalnya, persyaratan uji stabilitas mengikuti Pedoman Stabilitas ASEAN, sedangkan persyaratan uji klinis mengacu pada seri pedoman ICH (Efficacy).

The ACTD thus provides a format for structuring and presenting submission documents, rather than defining the product's assessment criteria. Applicants must complete the ACTD framework with comprehensive and relevant data to satisfy the requirements of the regulatory authorities.

Benefits and Objectives of Certification Implementation

The primary purpose of ACTD implementation is to enhance the efficiency and streamlining of the drug marketing authorization assessment process.

Previously, each ASEAN member state utilized disparate submission formats, leading companies to frequently re-prepare documentation for each country and thereby increasing administrative burden. The introduction of the common format ACTD enables companies to significantly reduce duplication of effort and accelerate time-to-market, ultimately providing patients across the ASEAN region with faster access to novel therapies.

For regulatory authorities, uniformity of format facilitates the review process and may reduce assessment timelines. Furthermore, inter-authority collaboration during the development of the ACTD strengthens information exchange and technical cooperation (e.g., GMP inspections and post-marketing surveillance).

Overall, ACTD helps lower approval acquisition costs and enhances the likelihood of successful multi-country submissions, while simultaneously driving improvements in documentation quality and the company's submission capabilities.

It should be noted that ACTD serves as a framework guideline, rather than a fully binding legal regulation in each country. Consequently, some additional national requirements may still apply, and applicants are required to adjust their documentation in accordance with local regulations.

Nevertheless, the initiative by regulatory authorities and industry to adopt a common format provides a crucial foundation for the harmonization of pharmaceutical regulations in ASEAN. For companies, preparing documentation in the ACTD format enables submission to multiple countries with minimal adjustments, thereby reducing submission costs and increasing the likelihood of approval.

Impact of Certification on Products, Services, and Consumers

Manfaat ACTD terutama terlihat pada proses internal bagi perusahaan farmasi dan regulator, namun efek akhirnya dirasakan oleh pasien dan konsumen. Dengan mempercepat dan menyederhanakan proses penilaian, ACTD membantu mempercepat ketersediaan obat baru dan generik di pasar ASEAN.

For instance, when an innovative drug is developed, disparities in submission formats across countries can lead to approval delays in some nations; the implementation of ACTD helps standardize these formats and minimize the disparity in approval times across countries.

Consequently, patients in ASEAN will gain expedited access to novel therapies, thereby contributing to greater equity in treatment opportunities across the region.

Furthermore, more precise and comprehensive submission documents are likely to indirectly enhance pharmaceutical quality and safety. During the process of data restructuring, companies frequently identify and rectify deficiencies or inconsistencies.

Consequently, the submitted documents become more credible, and inspections can be conducted with greater precision, ensuring that products on the market have undergone enhanced evaluation and verification, thereby fostering greater consumer confidence.

Furthermore, ACTD presents an opportunity for the introduction of the electronic Common Technical Document (eCTD) in the ASEAN region. With eCTD, the risk of errors or loss of physical documents is reduced, and interactions between authorities and applicants become more efficient.

This enables more rapid feedback on inspection results, streamlines the supply of pharmaceuticals to the market, and assists patients in obtaining necessary medications promptly.

Overall, while ACTD operates behind the scenes for consumers, its impact is evident in enhanced access to new medicines and increased confidence in the quality of available drugs.

International Organizations and Related Regulatory Authorities

The ACTD was developed within the framework of ASEAN (Association of Southeast Asian Nations) through cooperation within the ASEAN Working Group on Pharmaceutical Regulatory Harmonization. Representatives from regulatory authorities of member states—including Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Brunei, Vietnam, Laos, Myanmar, and Cambodia—were involved in the development of these guidelines.

As an official ASEAN document, the current ACTD guidelines are adopted and implemented as part of the pharmaceutical regulatory framework in several member states. For instance, authorities such as NPRA (Malaysia), FDA (Thailand), and BPOM (Indonesia) have issued provisions encouraging the use of ACTD-compliant formats in specific registration processes.

Internationally, the ACTD demonstrates strong alignment with the ICH CTD (International Council for Harmonisation — Common Technical Document). ICH is a pharmaceutical regulatory harmonization forum comprising stakeholders from Japan, the United States, and Europe; its resulting CTD has become a widely adopted reference in numerous jurisdictions.

The ACTD is based on the principles of the CTD but adapted to the ASEAN regional context. Therefore, it has significant alignment with the practices and requirements of authorities such as ICH, the EU, and US authorities (e.g., FDA). This makes documents prepared for the ASEAN market relatively easy to adapt for submission outside the region.

In addition, the ACTR (ASEAN Common Technical Requirements), a set of technical guidelines detailing the content and standards for specific sections such as stability testing and BA/BE studies, was also developed to complement the ACTD. Authorities in each country typically refer to both the ACTD and ACTR concurrently when conducting assessments, with the aim of reducing inter-country variations in interpretation.

Essentially, although ACTD is a regional initiative, it leverages international knowledge (e.g., ICH, WHO) and fosters information exchange among regulatory authorities. This makes ACTD a successful example of region-led regulatory harmonization.

    The ACTD adheres to the fundamental principles of the internationally recognized Common Technical Document (CTD), but is adapted to the regulatory requirements of the ASEAN region.

    The ACTD was introduced around 2009 as part of efforts to harmonize drug regulations in ASEAN. With the establishment of a common document format, it is expected that the efficiency of the assessment process will increase and the administrative burden on applicants will be reduced.

    Key Requirements and Structure

    Broadly, ACTD is structured into four main parts (Part I–IV), which respectively cover administrative, quality, non-clinical, and clinical aspects.

    Part I: Administrative Information and Products

    This section contains administrative information such as application forms, applicant information, product summary (name, dosage form, composition), labels and leaflets, list of manufacturers, and other legal documents as may be required by local authorities. Administrative details may differ across countries, therefore certain appendices must be adapted to local regulations.

    Part II: Quality (Chemical, Pharmaceutical)

    Part II contains CMC (Chemistry, Manufacturing and Controls) data, including active ingredient information, manufacturing process, formulation, test specifications, stability data, impurity profile, and other supporting documents demonstrating product quality.

    Part III: Non-clinical (Safety)

    This section presents preclinical study results, including toxicology, pharmacology, and pharmacokinetic studies, that support the product's safety profile.

    Part IV: Clinical (Efficacy)

    Part IV includes human clinical trial data (Phases I–III), summary reports, statistical analyses, and post-marketing information, where available. Its primary objective is to clinically demonstrate the product's safety and efficacy.

    The ACTD functions as a framework for organizing submission documents; specific technical guidelines (e.g., stability requirements or analytical methodologies) are typically regulated through supplementary guidelines (such as ACTR) or national regulations that refer to international standards like ICH.

    Benefits of ACTD Implementation

    The implementation of ACTD benefits applicants, regulators, and patients. For pharmaceutical companies, preparing documents in ACTD format reduces duplicated effort when submitting for approval to multiple ASEAN countries, thereby accelerating time to market.

    For regulatory authorities, standardized formats facilitate the review process and comparison across submissions. Collectively, this harmonization also fosters technical cooperation among authorities.

    The anticipated impact is expedited patient access to new drugs across the ASEAN region and enhanced quality of documentation submitted by the industry.

    Relations with International Organizations

    While ACTD is an ASEAN regional initiative, its formulation takes into account international CTD (ICH) principles and frequently references other global guidelines. National authorities such as BPOM (Indonesia), NPRA (Malaysia), and FDA (Thailand) have adopted ACTD in their evaluation processes.

    There is a supporting technical document called ACTR (ASEAN Common Technical Requirements) which details the technical requirements for each section of the ACTD, such as stability guidelines or BA/BE tests for the ASEAN region.

    Overall, the ACTD and related documents are part of a broader regulatory harmonization effort to enhance the quality and consistency of drug assessment in the ASEAN region.
