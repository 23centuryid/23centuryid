    Overview and Background

    • cGTP (current Good Tissue Practice) adalah standar yang mengatur pengelolaan produk berbasis sel dan jaringan manusia untuk memastikan keamanan dan kualitas mulai dari pengambilan donor hingga penggunaan klinis.

  • cGTP stands for "current Good Tissue Practice" and refers to regulatory standards developed by the FDA (Food and Drug Administration) in the United States. These standards specifically apply to the handling of human cells and tissues (HCT/P - Human Cells, Tissues, and cellular and tissue-based Products).

    Privacy Policy

    • The development of cGTP was prompted by the increasing demand for transplantation procedures and cell therapies, coupled with concerns regarding the potential transmission of infectious diseases through biological products. To prevent the spread of infections such as HIV, hepatitis, or other infectious agents from donor to recipient, the FDA established cGTP requirements in 2004, which became fully effective in 2005.

    Following the adoption of cGTP, institutions worldwide involved in the handling of human tissues and cells (particularly those collaborating with U.S. institutions) are also implementing similar principles. These standards serve as the foundation for safe practices in stem cell storage, transplant tissue processing, and the management of cell-based biotechnology products.

    Key Requirements and Standards

    Donor Eligibility Evaluation and Screening

    Donors of cells and tissues must undergo comprehensive screening, including a thorough review of their medical history and laboratory tests, to detect transmissible infections. Required tests include screening for HIV, hepatitis B, hepatitis C, syphilis, and other transmissible infectious agents. Donors identified as posing an infection risk must be excluded, and their products must not be used.

    Facilities and Environmental Control

    Tissue and cell processing facilities must be designed for aseptic (sterile) operations. These include clean rooms, biological safety cabinets, air filtration systems, and regular environmental monitoring through microbial testing. Equipment must be validated and regularly maintained to ensure optimal performance.

    Raw Material Management and Control

    Summary and Background

    All materials in contact with tissues and cells—including culture media, preservative solutions, containers, and labels—must undergo quality control and meticulous tracking. Each batch or lot of material must be documented and, in the event of an issue, traceable back to its source for corrective action.

    cGTP (current Good Tissue Practice) adalah standar yang mengatur pengelolaan produk berbasis sel dan jaringan manusia untuk memastikan keamanan dan kualitas mulai dari pengambilan donor hingga penggunaan klinis.

    Often compared to GMP for pharmaceuticals, cGTP focuses on specific requirements for the handling of human cells, tissues, and cellular and tissue-based products (HCT/P).

    The Process of Acquisition, Processing, Storage, and Delivery

    This regulation is enforced by regulatory bodies such as the FDA in the US to prevent disease transmission through biological products and ensure safe collection, processing, storage, and distribution practices.

    Tissue procurement must be performed aseptically (sterile). Following procurement, the tissue must be processed promptly under controlled conditions, such as washing, concentrating, or proper cryopreservation. During storage, temperature and humidity must be monitored and maintained. Shipment must be conducted using a well-maintained temperature-controlled system (cold chain).

    Key Requirements

    Quality Control and Product Testing

    Inti dari cGTP adalah pencegahan penularan penyakit menular. Persyaratan mencakup evaluasi kelayakan donor, kontrol lingkungan fasilitas, manajemen bahan baku dan reagen, serta dokumentasi dan pelacakan sepanjang proses.

    Each production stage must incorporate quality inspections and testing. These include, for example, sterility testing to ensure the absence of bacterial or fungal contamination, cell count and viability measurements, endotoxin testing, and other relevant assessments. All test results must be thoroughly documented and evaluated before the product is declared fit for use or commercial release.

    Donor Eligibility Assessment

    Documentation and Tracking

    Donors must undergo screening, including medical history assessment and laboratory testing, to exclude infectious risks like HIV or hepatitis.

    All processes shall be documented through written Standard Operating Procedures. Records for each product batch or lot must retain donor information, collection date, test results, storage details, and distribution records. This comprehensive traceability enables rapid investigation in the event of issues and facilitates product recall when necessary.

    Facilities and Environmental Control

    Personnel Training and Competency

    The facility must have a cleanroom and the capability for aseptic operations, including periodic environmental monitoring and equipment maintenance.

    All personnel involved in the handling of tissues and cells shall be adequately trained in aseptic procedures, biosafety, and specific cGTP requirements. Training shall be documented, and new personnel shall complete an orientation program prior to performing work independently.

    Materials and Process Management

    All materials coming into contact with tissue must undergo quality control and be traceable by batch. The processes of collection, processing, storage, and shipment must maintain conditions that minimize contamination.

    Significance and Benefits of Compliance

    Quality Control and Documentation

    For Institutions and Medical Facilities

    Every step must be documented in accordance with standard operating procedures and records, enabling traceability and corrective actions in the event of anomalies.

    Compliance with cGTP demonstrates an institution's commitment to safety and quality. This enhances trust among clinical partners, regulators, and, most importantly, patients. cGTP-compliant facilities can more easily engage in international partnerships, undergo regulatory audits more smoothly, and obtain certification from international bodies such as AABB or FACT.

    In summary, cGTP mitigates the risk of transmission via tissue and cell products by implementing consistent controls throughout the supply chain and production processes.

    For Patients and Recipients

    cGTP compliance directly protects patients by reducing the risk of disease transmission through transplantation or cell infusion. Patients who receive tissue or cells from cGTP-certified facilities can be assured that the products have been screened and processed to the highest international safety standards.

    Benefits of cGTP Certification

    Adherence to cGTP demonstrates a commitment to safety and quality, thereby enhancing the confidence of clinical partners, regulators, and patients.

    Impact on Products, Services, and Consumers

    Tissue and cell products processed in accordance with cGTP exhibit superior quality standards. The risk of contamination is substantially reduced, cell viability is better preserved, and clinical performance is enhanced. Successful transplantation or cell therapy with minimal complications leads to increased patient satisfaction and strengthens public confidence in regenerative medicine and cell therapy.

    Additionally, compliant facilities will be better positioned to participate in international cooperation and receive more favorable assessments during regulatory inspections.

    Across the broader biotechnology and medical industries, cGTP establishes a safer environment for consumers and drives continuous innovation in cell therapy and tissue regeneration.

    Organizational and Regulatory Matters

    Although cGTP is a concept pioneered by the U.S. FDA, its principles are shared internationally and are relevant to guidance from bodies such as the WHO, as well as certification standards from organizations like AABB and FACT.

    International Organizations and Related Regulatory Bodies

    FDA (Food and Drug Administration) - United States

    The FDA is the agency that establishes and oversees cGTP regulations in the United States. The Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research (CBER) division is responsible for supervising these standards and conducting facility inspections to ensure compliance. Violations of cGTP can result in serious legal penalties.

    World Health Organization (WHO)

    WHO issued guidance on the quality and safety of cells and tissues for developing countries, with principles aligned with cGTP.

    AABB (Association for the Advancement of Blood & Biotherapies)

    AABB offers an international certification program for facilities that handle blood, cell, and tissue products. AABB Standards largely incorporate cGTP principles and are often accepted as evidence of cGTP compliance.

    FACT (Foundation for Accreditation of Cellular Therapy)

    FACT provides accreditation for cell therapy and hematopoietic stem cell transplantation facilities, with requirements that align with or exceed cGTP.

    Other National Regulatory Body

    In Japan, the PMDA (Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency) implements similar standards. In Europe, the EMA (European Medicines Agency) also establishes comparable quality standards. This indicates that cGTP principles have become a global standard for the processing and management of biological cells and tissues.
